# Answers: Module 01 — Foundations and Workstation

## Answer Key

### Easy Questions
**Q1:** A feedback loop is the path from a change or action to evidence about its result.
**Q2:** A golden path is the recommended, supported workflow for a common engineering task.
**Q3:** It reveals uncommitted changes and prevents accidental automation over unknown state.
**Q4:** It proves the service endpoint is reachable and returning an expected successful response.
**Q5:** Examples include required tests, policy checks, least-privilege access, or deployment approvals.

### Medium Questions
**Q6:** Shared ownership reduces handoff failures because the people changing software also understand operational consequences.
**Q7:** Documentation makes intent and procedures understandable; automation makes repeated execution consistent.
**Q8:** Developers adopt platforms that solve real work with low friction, so platform teams need product empathy and feedback.
**Q9:** A runbook lowers stress and variance by giving responders a practiced sequence of checks and actions.
**Q10:** Standardization improves safety and speed but can constrain teams with unusual requirements.

### Hard Questions
**Q11:** A correct answer includes language-tagged YAML with test, build, deploy, and verify stages.
**Q12:** Record and expose the version, require immutable artifact identifiers, and log deployment metadata.
**Q13:** `curl -fsS "http://localhost:8080/health"` is a valid answer.
**Q14:** Strong answers justify improvements such as CI tests, deployment verification, rollback, observability, or release notes.

### Expert Questions
**Q15:** Strong answers identify a specific user and service type, then connect workflow, guardrails, and feedback signals.
**Q16:** Strong answers explain that automation changes systems, observability shows the effects, and incident response guides recovery.

### Bonus Questions
**Bonus 1:** Strong answers discuss issues like unclear ownership, incentives, trust, cognitive load, or poor communication.
