## Hard Exercises (7–8)

### Exercise 7

**Difficulty:** Hard
**Objective:** Apply the empowered product team model to a real-world scenario

**Scenario:** You are a PM at a company that sells project management software. Your CEO has just returned from a conference and sends you a Slack message: "I just saw a demo of CompetitorX's new AI-powered task assignment feature. We need to build the same thing. Please have an estimate from engineering by end of week and get it on the Q3 roadmap."

Write a 300–400 word response you would send to the CEO that:
1. Acknowledges the input seriously (do not dismiss it)
2. Explains what discovery work you'd want to do first before committing to a solution
3. Proposes a concrete next step (not "let's think about it")
4. Does this without making the CEO feel overruled or dismissed

This is a stakeholder management exercise as much as a product exercise. Your tone matters as much as your reasoning.

### Exercise 8

**Difficulty:** Hard
**Objective:** Critique a product decision using module frameworks

Below is a brief case study. Read it and then write a 400–500 word critical analysis using frameworks from this module (discovery vs. delivery, the three PM dimensions, the lifecycle model).

**Case study: Quibi**

Quibi was a mobile video streaming platform founded by Jeffrey Katzenberg and Meg Whitman, launched in April 2020 with $1.75 billion in funding. The core product insight was that people would watch high-quality, professionally produced, episodic content in 10-minute "quick bite" chunks on their phones. The company hired A-list directors and talent and produced high-quality programming.

Quibi shut down in December 2020, just 6 months after launch, having attracted only 500,000 paying subscribers against a target of 7.4 million for year one. Katzenberg attributed the failure partly to the COVID-19 pandemic (people were at home and preferred TV screens). However, others pointed to different factors: users couldn't share content from Quibi to social media (a discovery gap), the shows could only be watched in portrait orientation on phones (a usability gap discovered too late), and research suggested the "quick bites on mobile" use case was already served by YouTube and TikTok for free.

Your analysis should address:
- What discovery gaps likely contributed to the failure?
- Which of the three PM dimensions (desirability, viability, feasibility) were most underweighted?
- What could have been done differently in the discovery phase?

## Expert Exercise (9)

**Difficulty:** Expert
**Objective:** Synthesize module concepts into an original PM philosophy

**Prompt:** Write a 500–700 word "product management philosophy" — a personal statement of your beliefs about how good product management should be practiced. Your statement must:

1. Take a defensible position on at least three of the following tensions in PM practice:
   - Discovery depth vs. speed (how much validation is enough before building?)
   - User advocacy vs. business requirements (what do you do when user needs and business needs conflict?)
   - PM authority vs. team autonomy (how much should a PM decide unilaterally?)
   - Output metrics vs. outcome metrics (how do you manage a team that wants to measure what it ships?)
   - Short-term execution vs. long-term strategy (how do you balance quarterly goals with multi-year vision?)

2. Be grounded in at least two specific examples (real products, companies, or hypothetical scenarios)

3. Acknowledge the limitations of your own positions — where does your philosophy break down?

This exercise is open-ended by design. There is no single correct answer. You will be evaluated on the clarity of your reasoning, the specificity of your examples, and your ability to hold competing ideas simultaneously.
