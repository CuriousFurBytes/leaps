## Project Milestones as Exercises

Each milestone in the capstone README serves as an exercise. They are listed here for reference:

| Milestone | Description | Est. Hours |
|-----------|-------------|-----------|
| 1 | Problem hypothesis and research plan | 2–3 |
| 2 | User research (5 interviews + synthesis) | 4–6 |
| 3 | Validated problem statement and opportunity sizing | 1–2 |
| 4 | Product strategy one-pager | 2–3 |
| 5 | Prioritization (RICE/ICE) and MVP scope | 1–2 |
| 6 | OKRs and success metrics | 1 |
| 7 | 6-month outcome-based roadmap | 1–2 |
| 8 | GTM plan for MVP launch | 1–2 |
| 9 | Package assembly and reflection | 1–2 |

**Complete milestones in order.** Each one builds on the previous.

---

## Checkpoint Questions

Use these questions to evaluate your own work before moving to the next milestone:

**After Milestone 2 (research):**
- Could I describe the user's job in one clear sentence?
- Did any interview surprise me? If not, was I actually listening or just confirming?
- Are my "opportunities" truly user needs (not feature requests)?

**After Milestone 4 (strategy):**
- Can I complete this sentence without hesitation: "We are explicitly NOT building X because..."?
- Does my North Star metric measure user value or business revenue? (Should be user value)
- Could an engineer read my strategy and understand which problems are in scope vs. out?

**After Milestone 7 (roadmap):**
- Can I complete this sentence for each roadmap item: "We'll know this worked when [metric] moves from [baseline] to [target]"?
- Do the roadmap items connect back to specific opportunities from my research?
- Does the Now horizon feel genuinely minimal, or am I still trying to build everything?

**After Milestone 9 (reflection):**
- Am I being honest about what didn't work, or am I writing a success story?
- What would I tell my past self at the start of this project?
