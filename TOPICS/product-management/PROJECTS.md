# Product Management, Design and Discovery — Projects

> Project-based learning cements knowledge in a way that reading and exercises alone cannot.
> Applying PM frameworks to a real product problem forces you to confront gaps in your
> understanding and make decisions that tutorials never put in front of you.

---

## How to Use This File

1. Choose a project at or slightly above your current level.
2. **Don't use tutorials for your chosen project.** Apply the frameworks you've learned directly.
3. Use a real product idea if you have one — real stakes produce real learning.
4. Document your attempt using the [Project Attempt Template](#project-attempt-template) at the bottom.
5. When you finish, link the result in the Projects table in the topic README.

## Beginner Projects

_Focus: get comfortable with the core vocabulary and frameworks._
_Complete at least one before advancing past Phase 1._

### B1: PM Role Mapping

**Description:** Choose a product you use daily (could be an app, website, or software tool). Write a one-page analysis that answers: Who are the users? What problem does it solve? What do you think the North Star metric is? What would you guess the PM's top priority is right now?

**Concepts Reinforced:**
- PM role and responsibilities
- User vs. customer distinction
- North Star metric thinking
- Discovery vs. delivery framing

**Estimated Time:** 2–3 hours

**Starting Point:** Pick any product you use regularly. Write in prose or use a structured template.

**Success Criteria:**
- [ ] Clearly identifies at least two distinct user types with different needs
- [ ] Proposes a North Star metric and justifies the choice
- [ ] Identifies at least one tension the PM likely faces (e.g., enterprise vs. consumer users)
- [ ] Includes one question you'd want answered by talking to users

---

### B2: User Interview Practice

**Description:** Conduct 3 short (20-minute) user interviews about a product or problem area you're interested in. Use the interview guide from Module 02. Document your findings using Jobs-to-be-Done language.

**Concepts Reinforced:**
- Structured user interview technique
- Jobs-to-be-Done framing
- Synthesizing qualitative insights

**Estimated Time:** 4–6 hours (including recruiting, conducting, and synthesizing)

**Starting Point:** Pick a problem space (e.g., "how people manage their personal finances" or "how students organize their study materials"). Recruit 3 people who fit the target profile — friends, colleagues, or community members.

**Success Criteria:**
- [ ] All 3 interviews last ≥ 15 minutes
- [ ] Interviewer asked follow-up questions rather than leading questions
- [ ] Each interview is summarized with at least 3 key insights
- [ ] A synthesized "job statement" is written: "[verb] [object] in [context] so that [desired outcome]"

---

### B3: Opportunity Solution Tree Sketch

**Description:** For a product you know well, sketch an opportunity solution tree. Start from the product's business outcome (or North Star metric), branch into user opportunities, and list potential solutions under each opportunity.

**Concepts Reinforced:**
- Opportunity solution tree structure
- Distinguishing opportunities from solutions
- Connecting business outcomes to user needs

**Estimated Time:** 2–3 hours

**Starting Point:** A large piece of paper, a whiteboard, or a diagramming tool. Choose a product outcome to start from.

**Success Criteria:**
- [ ] Tree has at least one business outcome at the root
- [ ] At least 3 distinct opportunity nodes (not solutions)
- [ ] Each opportunity is framed as a user need or problem, not a feature request
- [ ] At least 2 solutions listed under each opportunity

## Intermediate Projects

_Focus: combine multiple frameworks, handle tradeoffs, make defensible decisions._
_Complete at least one before completing Phase 2._

### I1: Prioritization Exercise

**Description:** For a real or hypothetical product, assemble a list of 10–15 potential features or improvements. Apply RICE scoring to each. Write a one-page prioritization document that presents your ranked list and defends the top 3 priorities.

**Concepts Reinforced:**
- RICE scoring (Reach, Impact, Confidence, Effort)
- Distinguishing input metrics from outcome metrics
- Communicating prioritization decisions to stakeholders

**Estimated Time:** 4–6 hours

**Starting Point:** Choose a product you know or one from the case studies in Module 05. Invent plausible RICE inputs if you don't have real data — the exercise is in the reasoning, not the numbers.

**Success Criteria:**
- [ ] All 10–15 items have RICE scores with explicit estimates for each dimension
- [ ] Write-up explains why the top 3 items are the right focus
- [ ] At least one item that "feels important" is ranked low — and the write-up explains why
- [ ] The document is written as if presenting to a stakeholder who will push back

---

### I2: Outcome-Based Roadmap

**Description:** Create a 6-month outcome-based roadmap for a product. The roadmap should have three time horizons (Now/Next/Later), use outcomes (not features) as the primary organizing concept, and include a separate version for each of two different audiences: the engineering team and the executive team.

**Concepts Reinforced:**
- Outcome vs. output framing
- Roadmap communication for different audiences
- Connecting roadmap items to strategy

**Estimated Time:** 5–8 hours

**Starting Point:** Use a product from a previous exercise, or choose a well-known product. Use the roadmap templates in Module 06.

**Success Criteria:**
- [ ] Each roadmap item is expressed as an outcome ("reduce time-to-first-value") not a feature ("redesign onboarding")
- [ ] Engineering version includes technical context and dependencies
- [ ] Executive version includes business impact and strategic rationale
- [ ] Roadmap is defensible — you can explain why each item is in its time horizon

---

### I3: Go-to-Market Plan for a Feature

**Description:** For a significant feature (real or hypothetical), write a complete go-to-market plan. Include: target audience, positioning statement, key messages, launch tier (soft vs. broad), instrumentation plan, and rollout strategy.

**Concepts Reinforced:**
- GTM strategy structure
- Launch tiers and progressive rollout
- Positioning and messaging frameworks
- Success metric definition pre-launch

**Estimated Time:** 5–7 hours

**Success Criteria:**
- [ ] Positioning statement follows the [For... who... the product is a... that... unlike... our product...] framework
- [ ] Launch tier is explicitly chosen and justified
- [ ] At least 3 success metrics defined before launch, with targets
- [ ] Rollout plan includes a clear "kill switch" — what would cause you to halt or roll back the launch

## Advanced Projects

_Focus: tackle realistic, open-ended problems. No hand-holding._
_Complete at least one to reach Applied Practitioner status._

### A1: Full Discovery Sprint

**Description:** Conduct a week of discovery work on a real problem you care about. Conduct 5 user interviews, synthesize findings, build an opportunity solution tree, and write a problem statement + recommendation for what to build (and why not to build several alternatives you considered and rejected).

**Why This Is Hard:**
Real discovery requires recruiting real participants, tolerating ambiguous data, making judgment calls with incomplete information, and saying no to the solutions that feel obvious before the interviews.

**Concepts Reinforced:**
- Continuous discovery habits
- User interview synthesis
- Opportunity solution tree
- Problem framing
- Build/buy/no-build decision-making

**Estimated Time:** 10–15 hours

**Success Criteria:**
- [ ] 5 user interviews conducted with documented notes
- [ ] Findings synthesized into a clear opportunity landscape (not a list of feature requests)
- [ ] Opportunity solution tree covers at least 4 opportunities and 2 solutions per opportunity
- [ ] Write-up explicitly names 2–3 solutions considered and rejected, with reasoning
- [ ] You can explain every recommendation you made

---

### A2: Product Strategy Document

**Description:** Write a full product strategy document for a real product or your own product idea. Include: 5-year vision, 1-year strategy (how you win), 3 product bets, North Star metric, and a set of OKRs for the next quarter.

**Why This Is Hard:** Strategy requires making choices. A document that says "we will do everything" is not a strategy. The test is whether a reasonable person would read your strategy and say "this means they're choosing NOT to do X and Y."

**Concepts Reinforced:**
- Product vision vs. strategy distinction
- North Star metric definition
- OKR construction
- Strategic frameworks (Blue Ocean, Porter's 5 Forces)

**Estimated Time:** 8–12 hours

**Success Criteria:**
- [ ] Vision is aspirational and time-bound (5-year horizon)
- [ ] Strategy is explicit about what you are *not* doing
- [ ] North Star metric is a user-value metric (not a revenue metric)
- [ ] OKRs have measurable Key Results with specific targets and time horizons
- [ ] Document can withstand a "why?" question at each claim — every assertion is supported

## Expert / Capstone Projects

_Focus: synthesize everything. Build a complete product discovery and definition package._
_Complete one to earn Topic Mastery status._

### E1: Full Product Discovery and Definition Package

**Description:** Select a real product idea you believe is worth building. Over the course of several weeks, conduct the complete discovery-to-definition process: user research, problem framing, strategy, solution ideation, MVP definition, success metrics, OKRs, and a 6-month outcome-based roadmap.

**Why This Is a Capstone:**
This project requires mastery of all major topic areas. You cannot complete it with gaps — they will surface quickly. Expect to revisit earlier modules during the build. The final artifact should be something you could walk into a board meeting or investor conversation with.

**Core Requirements:**
- [ ] Uses concepts from at least 8 different modules
- [ ] Includes at least 5 documented user interviews with synthesized insights
- [ ] Includes a validated problem statement with opportunity sizing
- [ ] Includes a product strategy document (vision, strategy, bets, North Star metric)
- [ ] Includes a prioritized solution hypothesis with RICE scoring
- [ ] Includes a 6-month outcome-based roadmap
- [ ] Includes OKRs for the first quarter
- [ ] Includes a GTM plan for the first release
- [ ] Is documented clearly enough that someone else could understand and critique it
- [ ] Includes a write-up explaining your major decisions and what you learned

**Estimated Time:** 15–25 hours

**Extension Ideas (if you want more challenge):**
- Present the package to 3 people and incorporate their feedback into a revised version
- Create a Figma prototype for the MVP and run a usability test with 3 users
- Write a "press release" for the product in Amazon's Working Backwards style

> [!NOTE]
> The final module of this topic (Module 12) is a dedicated **Capstone Project** module that walks you
> through this project with a brief, milestones, and a **Help / Getting Unstuck** section with staged hints.
> It deliberately does **not** hand you a finished solution. The help is there so you can get past a blocker
> and keep going on your own. Struggling productively *is* the learning here.
