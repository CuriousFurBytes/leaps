## Why Learn French?

French is a global language used in Europe, Africa, North America, the Caribbean, diplomacy, literature, film, food, science, and international organizations. Learning it gives you access to communities and texts that are difficult to experience through translation alone.

French is also a strong language-learning laboratory because it forces attention to sound, register, grammar, and culture at the same time. A learner must connect pronunciation, spelling, politeness, verb forms, and context instead of memorizing isolated word lists.

This topic starts at absolute zero and ends with expert-level communicative judgment: choosing appropriate register, sustaining conversation, reading authentic texts, writing for a real audience, and repairing misunderstandings gracefully.

## Quick Reference

| Function | French | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Greet formally | Bonjour, Madame / Monsieur. | Use in shops, offices, and first meetings. |
| Greet informally | Salut ! | Use with friends or peers in casual settings. |
| Say thanks | Merci. | Add beaucoup for emphasis. |
| Ask for repetition | Vous pouvez répéter, s'il vous plaît ? | Formal and useful when stuck. |
| Say you do not understand | Je ne comprends pas encore. | Add encore to frame it as temporary. |
| Order politely | Je voudrais un café, s'il vous plaît. | Conditional form sounds courteous. |

```text
Bonjour. Je m'appelle Alex.
Je suis débutant en français.
Vous pouvez parler lentement, s'il vous plaît ?
```

## Study Method

Use a daily loop: listen, imitate, recall, speak, read, write, and reflect. Prioritize short, frequent practice over rare long sessions, and keep evidence of progress in module notes, exercises, recordings, and conversations.
