## Overview

Pronouns are what make French sentences flow rather than repeat noun phrases constantly. Without pronouns, every sentence would be laborious — "I spoke to Marie, I gave Marie the book, Marie thanked me." With pronouns: "Je lui ai parlé, je lui ai donné le livre, elle m'a remercié." 

French pronouns are more complex than English ones because they distinguish between direct and indirect objects, occupy a fixed pre-verbal position, must agree with the noun they replace in gender and number, and follow strict stacking rules when multiple pronouns appear. This module builds the complete system.

## Theory

### Subject Pronouns and the Informal *on*

The standard subject pronouns are covered in Module 04. The important addition here is **on**:

*On* officially means "one" (as in English "one says") but in everyday spoken French, it functions as an informal "we" — far more common than *nous* in conversation:

```text
On va au cinéma ce soir ?      Shall we go to the cinema tonight?
On est français.               We are French. (informal)
On y va !                      Let's go! (literally "one goes there")
```

*On* takes 3rd person singular verb forms: *on parle, on mange, on est*.

### Direct Object Pronouns (COD)

A direct object is a noun that receives the action of the verb directly, without a preposition. COD pronouns replace it:

```text
COD pronouns:  me (m'), te (t'), le (l'), la (l'), nous, vous, les

Je vois Marie.            → Je la vois.          (la replaces Marie, fem.)
Il mange le gâteau.       → Il le mange.          (le replaces le gâteau, masc.)
Elles nous appellent.     → (nous in subject → nous in COD; no change here — context clarifies)
Tu me connais ?           Do you know me?
```

**Position:** COD pronouns go **before the conjugated verb** (or before *être* in passé composé, or before the infinitive if it is the main verb):

```text
Je le mange.              (présent — before conjugated verb)
Je ne le mange pas.       (negation wraps around ne + pronoun + verb + pas)
Je l'ai mangé.            (passé composé — before avoir)
Je vais le manger.        (futur proche — before infinitive)
```

### Indirect Object Pronouns (COI)

An indirect object is a noun introduced by *à* (to/for someone). COI pronouns replace *à + person*:

```text
COI pronouns:  me (m'), te (t'), lui, nous, vous, leur

Je parle à Marie.         → Je lui parle.         (lui = à Marie)
Elle donne un livre à ses enfants. → Elle leur donne un livre.  (leur = à ses enfants)
```

**COD vs. COI — the key distinction:**
- **COD:** no preposition needed before the noun → use le/la/les
- **COI:** the noun follows *à* (to/for) → use lui/leur

The verbs that take COI include: *parler à, téléphoner à, donner à, écrire à, répondre à, dire à, demander à, montrer à, expliquer à, promettre à*

### Adverbial Pronouns: y and en

**y** — replaces *à + location* or *à + non-person noun* after certain verbs:

```text
Je vais à Paris.          → J'y vais.             (y = à Paris)
Elle pense à ce problème. → Elle y pense.          (y = à ce problème)
On y va !                 Let's go (there)!
```

**en** — replaces *de + noun* or a quantity expression:

```text
Tu viens de Paris ?       → Tu en viens ?          (en = de Paris)
Je mange des pommes.      → J'en mange.            (en = des pommes)
Tu veux du pain ?         → Oui, j'en veux.        (en replaces du pain)
Il a trois enfants.       → Il en a trois.         (en + the number)
```

### Pronoun Stacking Order

When multiple pronouns appear before the verb, they follow a strict order:

```text
ne + [me/te/se/nous/vous] + [le/la/les] + [lui/leur] + [y] + [en] + verb + pas
```

In practice, the most common combinations:

```text
Je te le donne.                I give it to you.
Il me l'a expliqué.            He explained it to me.
Elle le lui a montré.          She showed it to him/her.
Nous leur en avons parlé.      We spoke to them about it.
```

> [!WARNING]
> *me/te* cannot appear alongside *le/la/les* if the me/te IS the indirect object and le/la/les is the direct object — French reorders them: the COD comes first (le/la/les) and the COI comes second (lui/leur), unless both are 1st/2nd person, in which case 1st/2nd person comes first.

### Relative Pronouns

Relative pronouns introduce subordinate clauses that give more information about a noun (the antecedent):

**qui** — subject of the relative clause:
```text
L'homme qui parle est mon père.    The man who is speaking is my father.
J'ai un ami qui parle russe.       I have a friend who speaks Russian.
```

**que** — direct object of the relative clause:
```text
Le livre que je lis est intéressant.    The book (that) I am reading is interesting.
La femme que tu connais est partie.     The woman (that) you know has left.
```

**dont** — replaces *de + noun* in the relative clause:
```text
Le film dont je te parle est fantastique.    The film I'm telling you about is fantastic.
(dont = de ce film; parler de quelque chose → parler dont...)
```

**où** — relative pronoun for place or time:
```text
La ville où j'habite s'appelle Lyon.    The city where I live is called Lyon.
Le jour où je suis né...                The day (when) I was born...
```

### Stressed (Disjunctive) Pronouns

Used after prepositions, for emphasis, in comparisons, and in isolation:

```text
moi, toi, lui, elle, nous, vous, eux, elles
```

Uses:
```text
Avec moi. / Chez toi. / Pour lui.    (after prepositions)
Moi, je ne sais pas.                  (emphasis — contrast with subject pronoun)
Elle est plus grande que lui.          (comparisons)
Qui a fait ça ? — Moi !               (isolation)
C'est moi qui ai fait ça.             (c'est + stressed pronoun + qui)
```

## Key Concepts

**COD vs. COI distinction drives pronoun choice.** The verb's construction determines which type of object it takes. *Voir Marie* (see Marie — no preposition → COD → la voir). *Parler à Marie* (speak to Marie — à → COI → lui parler). This distinction must be checked per verb.

**y and en avoid repeating prepositional phrases.** Mastering *y* and *en* makes your French much more natural. Without them, French speakers would have to constantly repeat *de/à + noun* phrases.

**Relative pronoun choice depends on grammatical function, not meaning.** *Qui* = subject, *que* = direct object, *dont* = de + noun, *où* = place/time. The choice is not about what the pronoun "means" — it is about what grammatical role the antecedent plays in the subordinate clause.

## Examples

### Example 1: Replacing Objects with Pronouns

```text
Original:    J'ai donné le livre à Marie hier.
Step 1 (COD): J'ai donné le livre à Marie → replace le livre (COD) → Je l'ai donné à Marie.
Step 2 (COI): J'ai donné à Marie → replace à Marie (COI) → Je lui ai donné.
Step 3 (both): Je le lui ai donné hier.    (COD le before COI lui)

I gave her the book yesterday. → I gave it to her yesterday.
```

### Example 2: Relative Clauses in Context

```text
C'est le collègue dont je t'ai parlé hier.
That's the colleague I told you about yesterday.
(dont replaces de ce collègue; parler de quelqu'un → dont)

Je cherche la clé que j'ai perdue.
I'm looking for the key I lost.
(que = la clé, direct object of ai perdue)

Le quartier où j'habite est très calme.
The neighborhood where I live is very quiet.
```

## Common Pitfalls

**Pitfall 1: Using *lui/leur* (COI) for inanimate objects.**

*Lui* and *leur* only replace *à + person*. For *à + thing*, use *y*:
```text
Wrong:   Je pense à mon examen → Je lui pense.
Correct: Je pense à mon examen → J'y pense.
```

**Pitfall 2: Putting the pronoun after the verb.**

```text
Wrong:   Je mange-le. / J'ai le mangé.
Correct: Je le mange. / Je l'ai mangé.
```

**Pitfall 3: Using *que* for the subject of the relative clause.**

```text
Wrong:   L'homme que parle... (que cannot be subject)
Correct: L'homme qui parle... (qui is the subject)
```

## Summary

- COD pronouns (me, te, le, la, nous, vous, les) replace direct objects; go before the conjugated verb
- COI pronouns (me, te, lui, nous, vous, leur) replace *à + person* indirect objects
- Adverbial pronouns: *y* replaces *à + location/thing*; *en* replaces *de + noun* or quantity
- Pronoun order: me/te/se/nous/vous → le/la/les → lui/leur → y → en
- Relative pronouns: *qui* (subject), *que* (direct object), *dont* (de + noun), *où* (place/time)
- Stressed pronouns (moi, toi, lui, etc.): used after prepositions, for emphasis, in comparisons
- *On* = informal "we" in spoken French, very common
