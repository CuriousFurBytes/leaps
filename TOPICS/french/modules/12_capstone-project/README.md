## Project Brief

This is the final module of the French Language Learning curriculum. It has no new theory — its purpose is to synthesize everything you have learned in Modules 01–11 into a substantial, real-world linguistic performance.

You will produce **two artifacts**:

1. **An oral presentation** (5–7 minutes, to be delivered aloud and self-recorded if possible, or written as a full speech script) on a topic of your choice
2. **A formal essay** (400 words, ±5%) following the *dissertation* structure from Module 11, on a related or different topic from the presentation

Both artifacts must demonstrate:
- Correct and varied grammar across all tenses and moods learned (at minimum: présent, passé composé, imparfait, futur, conditionnel, subjonctif)
- Appropriate formal register (no informal contractions, no anglicisms unless discussing anglicisms, no first-person *je* in the essay)
- Varied vocabulary with no excessive repetition
- Correct pronunciation (for the oral) and correct spelling with accents (for the essay)
- Cultural or factual accuracy appropriate to the topic

> [!IMPORTANT]
> This project must be your own work. AI tools may be used to check your grammar after you have written the draft — not to generate the draft for you. The goal is to discover what you can actually produce in French. A short, honest, imperfect text you wrote yourself demonstrates more genuine learning than a polished text generated for you.

## Milestones

Work through these milestones in sequence. Do not skip ahead.

### Milestone 1: Choose Your Topics

**By:** Start of capstone work

Choose your oral presentation topic and your essay topic. They may be related or completely different. Guidelines:

- Choose something you genuinely find interesting — you will spend significant time on it
- The topic must be substantive enough to support 5–7 minutes of structured content
- You must be able to discuss it accurately — do not choose a topic where you lack the knowledge to speak confidently

**Topic ideas (not requirements — any topic is acceptable):**

*Oral presentation:*
- A francophone country or region you are interested in
- A period of French history (the Revolution, the Occupation, May 1968)
- A French cultural figure (filmmaker, author, musician, philosopher)
- A contemporary social or political issue in France or the francophone world
- A comparison between French culture and your own

*Formal essay:*
- The role of French in the 21st century
- Pros and cons of a social/technological development
- A question of education, environment, urbanism, or health in French-speaking countries
- A classic French dissertation topic: liberty vs. security, individual vs. society, tradition vs. modernity

**Submit:** Write the two topics in your NOTES.md.

---

### Milestone 2: Research and Outline

**By:** After choosing topics

For the oral presentation:
- Read at least 2 sources in French on your topic (TV5Monde, RFI, Le Monde — see RESOURCES.md)
- Create a detailed outline with the structure from Module 11: introduction, 2–3 developed sections, conclusion
- List 10–15 key vocabulary items specific to your topic that you will need

For the essay:
- Identify the *problématique* (central question) you will address
- Draft an outline: accroche, mise en contexte, problématique, annonce du plan, 2–3 développement sections, conclusion
- Identify the grammatical structures you intend to use (plan to include: subjunctive, conditional, relative clauses, past tenses)

**Submit:** Outline for both artifacts in your NOTES.md.

---

### Milestone 3: First Drafts

**By:** After completing outlines

Write a complete first draft of both artifacts without stopping to look up every word. Work at your current level. Mark gaps with [?] where you need vocabulary or are uncertain of a construction. Aim for completeness over perfection.

For the oral presentation: write it out as a full script, then read it aloud at least once to time it. If it runs under 4 minutes, expand. If over 8, trim.

---

### Milestone 4: Revision

**By:** After completing first drafts

Revise each artifact:

1. Grammar check: check every verb tense and mood, every adjective agreement, every article usage
2. Vocabulary check: replace any English-influenced choices, look up [?] gaps
3. Register check: ensure no *je* in the essay, no informal contractions, appropriate connectors
4. Variety check: count how many times you used your most common verb and connector — replace some with synonyms
5. Read the essay aloud — if a sentence sounds wrong, it probably is

---

### Milestone 5: Final Production and Self-Assessment

**By:** After revision

Produce your final versions:

- **Oral:** Deliver your presentation aloud 3 times. If possible, record the final delivery. Note: it is better to deliver with notes than to read a script — but a delivered script is acceptable at this stage.
- **Essay:** Submit the final 400-word essay.

Complete the self-assessment rubric below before having your work graded.

---

## Oral Presentation Brief

### Requirements

- Duration: 5–7 minutes when delivered aloud
- Structure: follows Module 11's oral presentation framework (introduction with plan announcement, developed sections with transitions, conclusion with synthesis and opening)
- Content: factually accurate, demonstrating research
- Language: C1-level grammar, vocabulary, and pronunciation
- Delivery: from notes (not read word-for-word from script); eye contact and clear diction if recorded

### What Will Be Assessed

| Criterion | What It Means |
|-----------|--------------|
| Grammatical range | Use of multiple tenses and moods, including subjunctive and conditional |
| Vocabulary range | Absence of repetition; appropriate register; topic-specific vocabulary |
| Discourse structure | Clear introduction, developed body, conclusion |
| Pronunciation | Correct nasal vowels, liaison, elision, stress |
| Fluency | Natural pace; appropriate fillers; no excessive hesitation |

---

## Formal Essay Brief

### Requirements

- Length: 400 words (±5% = 380–420 words)
- Structure: dissertation format from Module 11
- Register: formal — no *je*, no informal contractions, no anglicisms
- Grammar: must include at minimum: one subjunctive, one conditional, one relative clause with *dont* or *que*, one past-tense passage (PC or imparfait), one complex connector (cependant, néanmoins, en revanche, or similar)
- Spelling: all accents correct; all agreement correct

### Self-Assessment Rubric (complete before submission)

Before submitting, verify:

- [ ] Word count is between 380 and 420 words
- [ ] Structure: I can identify my *accroche*, *problématique*, *annonce du plan*, 2–3 body sections, and conclusion
- [ ] I used *on/nous/il convient de* rather than *je* throughout
- [ ] I used at least 5 different discourse connectors
- [ ] I can identify at least one subjunctive construction
- [ ] I can identify at least one conditional construction
- [ ] I can identify at least one relative clause with *dont* or *que*
- [ ] I used passé composé or imparfait appropriately for any past-tense content
- [ ] I read the essay aloud once and corrected sentences that "sounded wrong"
- [ ] All adjectives agree with their nouns in gender and number
- [ ] All accent marks are present and correct

## Acceptance Criteria

The capstone is considered complete when:

1. Both artifacts (oral presentation + essay) are submitted in NOTES.md or linked files
2. The self-assessment rubric is completed honestly
3. An AI grading session (or self-grading against the rubric) has produced feedback
4. A written reflection (100–150 words in French) has been added to NOTES.md: what you found most challenging, what you are most proud of, and what you would study next

## Help and Getting Unstuck

> [!IMPORTANT]
> Try each level of help in sequence. Reaching for the final hints too quickly bypasses the most valuable part of the project — the productive struggle.

<details>
<summary>Help Level 1: I don't know what topic to choose</summary>

Start with something simple: describe your city, your work, your country, or a recent trip. A short, honest, well-structured presentation on a simple topic demonstrates more genuine French than a halting, vocabulary-strained attempt on a complex one. Ambition is good — overstretch is counterproductive at this stage.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Help Level 2: My essay is too short / I don't have enough to say</summary>

Use the classic technique of expanding each argument: (1) state the argument, (2) explain it, (3) give a specific example, (4) draw the conclusion. Each step adds words and depth. If your development section has only one sentence, apply all four steps to expand it to a paragraph.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Help Level 3: I keep making grammar errors I can't identify</summary>

Use a structured grammar-checking method:

1. Underline every verb. Verify: correct tense? correct form? correct auxiliary?
2. Circle every adjective. Verify: correct gender? correct number? correct position?
3. Box every article. Verify: definite vs. indefinite vs. partitive? negation rule applied?
4. Mark every connector. Verify: correct meaning? correct punctuation around it?

This systematic check finds 80% of errors that a general re-read misses.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Help Level 4: I'm stuck on the oral — I freeze when speaking</summary>

Freezing in an oral is usually caused by trying to retrieve words that are not yet automatic. Two solutions: (1) reduce the vocabulary load — use simpler words you know rather than complex words you are uncertain of; (2) prepare rescue phrases for when you lose the thread: *Ce que je voulais dire...* (What I wanted to say was...), *Pour reformuler...* (To rephrase...), *Permettez-moi de préciser...* (Allow me to clarify...). These signal to the listener that you are handling the language actively, not struggling — which is itself a mark of fluency.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Help Level 5: I want to verify my grammar before final submission</summary>

At this stage, AI grammar assistance is appropriate. Ask an AI to:
- Check all verb forms for correctness
- Flag adjective agreement errors
- Identify any article usage errors
- Point out any register inconsistencies (informal language in formal text)

Do NOT ask an AI to rewrite sentences for you — only to flag errors and explain why they are wrong. You then correct them yourself. This preserves the authenticity of your work while providing targeted feedback.

</details>

## Assessment

Your capstone will be assessed on the following rubric:

| Criterion | Weight | What It Measures |
|-----------|--------|-----------------|
| Grammatical accuracy | 25% | Correct verb forms, agreement, article usage |
| Grammatical range | 20% | Variety of tenses, moods, and structures deployed |
| Vocabulary range and accuracy | 20% | Appropriate vocabulary; no repetition; register correct |
| Discourse structure | 20% | Clear, explicit, logical organization of content |
| Task achievement | 15% | Essay meets word count; oral meets duration; both address the chosen topic coherently |

**Passing:** 70% overall
