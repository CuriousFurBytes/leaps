## Overview

This module introduces three new tense/mood forms: the **futur proche** (near future, formed with *aller + infinitif*), the **futur simple** (simple future), and the **conditionnel présent** (conditional — used for hypothetical situations, polite requests, and reported speech). Together with the si-clause structure, these give you the ability to discuss plans, predictions, desires, and hypothetical scenarios — a major step toward B1 communication.

## Theory

### Futur Proche (Near Future)

The easiest future construction: *aller* (présent) + infinitive of main verb.

```text
Je vais partir.          I am going to leave.
Tu vas manger ?          Are you going to eat?
Elle va finir ce soir.   She is going to finish tonight.
Nous allons voir ça.     We are going to see about that.
```

**Use:** For imminent or planned future actions, especially in spoken French. More common than futur simple in casual speech for near-term plans.

**Negation:** *ne + verb (aller) + pas + infinitive*: *Je ne vais pas partir* (I'm not going to leave).

### Futur Simple

**Formation:** Infinitive + futur endings. For -RE verbs, drop the final *-e* first.

Futur endings: **-ai, -as, -a, -ons, -ez, -ont** (note: these are forms of *avoir* in the présent — a helpful mnemonic).

```text
parler → je parlerai, tu parleras, il parlera, nous parlerons, vous parlerez, ils parleront
finir  → je finirai, tu finiras, il finira...
vendre → je vendrai, tu vendras... (drop -e from vendre)
```

**Irregular futur stems (same list as conditionnel):**

```text
être    → ser-:     je serai
avoir   → aur-:     j'aurai
aller   → ir-:      j'irai
faire   → fer-:     je ferai
venir   → viendr-:  je viendrai
pouvoir → pourr-:   je pourrai
vouloir → voudr-:   je voudrai
savoir  → saur-:    je saurai
voir    → verr-:    je verrai
devoir  → devr-:    je devrai
tenir   → tiendr-:  je tiendrai
falloir → faudr-:   il faudra
```

**Use of futur simple:**
- More formal than futur proche in speech
- For predictions, future facts, instructions, and conditions
- In si-clauses with si + présent: *Si tu travailles bien, tu réussiras.*

### Conditionnel Présent

**Formation:** Same stem as futur simple + imparfait endings (-ais, -ais, -ait, -ions, -iez, -aient).

```text
parler: je parlerais, tu parlerais, il parlerait, nous parlerions, vous parleriez, ils parleraient
être:   je serais, tu serais, il serait...
avoir:  j'aurais, tu aurais, il aurait...
```

**Uses of the conditionnel:**

1. **Hypothetical (si + imparfait → conditionnel):**
   ```text
   Si j'avais de l'argent, je voyagerais.
   If I had money, I would travel.
   ```

2. **Polite requests (the "conditional of politeness"):**
   ```text
   Je voudrais un café, s'il vous plaît.  (I would like a coffee — much more polite than je veux)
   Pourriez-vous répéter ?  (Could you repeat?)
   Auriez-vous un stylo ?  (Would you have a pen?)
   ```

3. **Reported speech in the past (future → conditional in indirect speech):**
   ```text
   Il a dit qu'il viendrait.  (He said he would come.)
   J'ai pensé que tu arriverais.  (I thought you would arrive.)
   ```

4. **Suggestions with *devoir* or *pouvoir*:**
   ```text
   Tu devrais partir maintenant.  (You should leave now.)
   On pourrait aller au cinéma.  (We could go to the cinema.)
   ```

### Si-Clauses

French si-clauses follow strict tense rules depending on the type of condition:

**Type 1: Open condition (possible/likely)**
```text
Structure: si + présent → futur simple (main clause)

Si tu travailles bien, tu réussiras.
If you work well, you will succeed.

Si elle arrive tard, nous commencerons sans elle.
If she arrives late, we will start without her.
```

**Type 2: Hypothetical (unlikely or unreal in present)**
```text
Structure: si + imparfait → conditionnel présent

Si j'avais le temps, je te téléphonerai*s*.
If I had time, I would call you.

Si nous étions riches, nous voyagerions.
If we were rich, we would travel.
```

**Type 3: Contrary to past fact (counterfactual)**
```text
Structure: si + plus-que-parfait → conditionnel passé

Si tu avais étudié, tu aurais réussi.
If you had studied, you would have succeeded.
```

(Type 3 is introduced here for awareness but covered fully in Module 09.)

> [!IMPORTANT]
> Never use a future or conditional tense immediately after *si* (when si = "if"). This is one of the most common errors English speakers make because English uses "if you will" in some constructions. In French: *si + présent* (not si + futur), *si + imparfait* (not si + conditionnel).

### Futur Antérieur (Brief Introduction)

The futur antérieur is formed with the future of *être/avoir* + past participle. It means "will have done" — a future action that will be completed before another future event.

```text
Quand tu arriveras, j'aurai déjà mangé.
When you arrive, I will have already eaten.

Elle partira dès qu'elle aura fini son travail.
She will leave as soon as she has finished her work.
```

It is also used to express a presumed past action:
```text
Il n'est pas là — il sera parti.
He isn't there — he must have left. (supposition about past)
```

## Key Concepts

**Futur proche vs. futur simple:** In informal spoken French, futur proche (*je vais parler*) is used for almost all near-future events. Futur simple (*je parlerai*) is more formal, used in writing, and for distant or predicted future events. Both are correct; register and context determine the choice.

**Conditionnel as politeness register.** The conditionnel is the standard French politeness strategy for requests. *Je veux* (I want) sounds blunt in a restaurant or shop; *Je voudrais* (I would like) is the expected form. *Pouvez-vous* (can you) is appropriate; *Pourriez-vous* (could you) is more deferential.

**Si-clause tense agreement is rigid.** Unlike English, which allows several variations ("if you would just..."), French si-clauses follow the rule strictly: si + présent/futur/futur antérieur (never conditional); si + imparfait (type 2); si + plus-que-parfait (type 3).

## Examples

### Example 1: Planning a Trip

```text
L'année prochaine, je vais visiter la France.    (futur proche — imminent plan)
J'irai d'abord à Paris, puis à Lyon.              (futur simple — sequence of planned events)
Si je peux, je resterai un mois.                  (si + présent → futur: open condition)
Ça serait magnifique !                            (conditionnel: hypothetical appreciation)
```

### Example 2: Polite Requests in a Restaurant

```text
Vous avez une table pour deux ?  (direct question)
Je voudrais voir la carte, s'il vous plaît.      (conditionnel = polite request)
Pourriez-vous nous apporter de l'eau ?           (conditionnel = polite request, formal)
Ce serait possible d'avoir une table en terrasse ? (conditionnel = polite enquiry)
```

## Common Pitfalls

**Pitfall 1: Using futur or conditionnel after *si* (if).**

```text
Wrong:   Si tu viendras, on mangera ensemble.
Correct: Si tu viens, on mangera ensemble. (si + présent → futur)

Wrong:   Si j'aurais le temps, je viendrais.
Correct: Si j'avais le temps, je viendrais. (si + imparfait → conditionnel)
```

**Pitfall 2: Confusing futur proche negation.**

```text
Wrong:   Je vais ne pas partir.
Correct: Je ne vais pas partir.  (ne...pas wraps around aller, not the infinitive)
```

**Pitfall 3: Using *je veux* instead of *je voudrais* in service contexts.**

```text
Less polite: Je veux un café.          (I want a coffee — abrupt)
More polite: Je voudrais un café.      (I would like a coffee — standard)
Very polite: Auriez-vous un café ?     (Would you have a coffee? — very deferential)
```

## Summary

- Futur proche: *aller* (présent) + infinitive — used for near/planned future in spoken French
- Futur simple: infinitive + endings (-ai, -as, -a, -ons, -ez, -ont); irregular stems for common verbs
- Conditionnel: futur stem + imparfait endings; used for hypotheticals, polite requests, reported speech
- Si-clauses: si + présent → futur (Type 1); si + imparfait → conditionnel (Type 2)
- Never use futur or conditionnel immediately after *si* (if)
- *Je voudrais* (conditionnel of vouloir) is the standard polite request form
- Futur antérieur: futur of avoir/être + past participle = "will have done"
