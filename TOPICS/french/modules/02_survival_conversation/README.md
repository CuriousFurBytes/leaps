## Overview

Handle practical first conversations: introductions, courtesy, numbers, time, directions, and ordering. The module emphasizes usable French, accurate habits, and confidence with small interactions before moving into larger communication tasks.

French learning works best when sound, meaning, and context are learned together. Every phrase here should be spoken aloud, listened to, and reused in a tiny personal scenario.

## Theory

### Core Mental Model

Survival conversation is the bridge between isolated phrases and real interaction. French speakers expect greetings, politeness markers, and clear openings before the practical request. This module teaches reusable frames so you can enter shops, ask for help, introduce yourself, count, and recover when you miss something.

### Historical and Cultural Context

Modern standard French grew from regional spoken Latin varieties and was later standardized through schools, printing, administration, and cultural institutions. This history explains why spelling can look conservative while everyday spoken French changes quickly across regions and generations.

### Practical Patterns

Use short patterns as flexible building blocks. Do not wait until you know every rule; speak with controlled phrases, listen for feedback, and revise.

```text
A: Bonjour, je voudrais un thé, s'il vous plaît.
B: Bien sûr. Sur place ou à emporter ?
A: À emporter, merci.
```

```text
un, deux, trois, quatre, cinq
six, sept, huit, neuf, dix
```

```text
Excusez-moi, où est la gare ?
C'est à droite, puis tout droit.
Merci beaucoup !
```

## Key Concepts

- **Input:** French you hear or read. Good input is understandable enough that you can attach meaning to form.
- **Output:** French you say or write. Output exposes gaps and turns passive recognition into active skill.
- **Register:** The level of formality in a phrase, such as tu versus vous.
- **Pronunciation target:** A sound pattern you deliberately practice until it becomes easier to hear and produce.
- **Repair phrase:** A phrase used to keep communication going when you are confused or searching for words.

## Examples

### Scenario: Introduce Yourself Simply

```text
Bonjour. Je m'appelle Sam.
Je suis débutant.
Je parle un peu français.
```

Use this when meeting a tutor, classmate, or exchange partner. It is short, honest, and gives the listener enough information to adjust their speed.

### Scenario: Ask for Slower Speech

```text
Vous pouvez parler plus lentement, s'il vous plaît ?
```

This phrase protects the conversation. It is better to ask for adjustment than to pretend you understood.

## Common Pitfalls

### Pitfall 1: Translating Word for Word

Wrong:

```text
Je suis vingt ans.
```

Correct:

```text
J'ai vingt ans.
```

French often uses different structures than English; learn phrases as patterns, not word substitutions.

### Pitfall 2: Dropping Politeness

Wrong:

```text
Un café.
```

Correct:

```text
Je voudrais un café, s'il vous plaît.
```

Politeness frames are part of the interaction, especially with strangers.

### Pitfall 3: Avoiding Speech Until Perfect

Wrong:

```text
I will speak after I memorize all the grammar.
```

Correct:

```text
Bonjour. Je suis débutant, mais je veux pratiquer.
```

Early speaking builds listening, memory, and confidence when it stays small and respectful.
