## Overview

Verbs are the engine of the French sentence. This module establishes your command of the present tense (*présent de l'indicatif*) — the tense you will use most in early learning to describe current states, habitual actions, and immediate plans. The present tense in French covers a wider range than English: *Je parle* can mean "I speak," "I am speaking," or even "I do speak."

The module begins with *être* and *avoir* — the two foundational verbs of French. They are not only the most frequently used verbs in the language but also the auxiliary verbs (helpers) that form compound tenses, making them essential to master before anything else. The module then covers the three regular verb groups and 15 key irregular verbs that appear in daily communication.

## Objectives

By the end of this module, you will be able to:

1. Conjugate *être* and *avoir* in the present tense from memory
2. Conjugate any regular *-ER*, *-IR*, and *-RE* verb in the present tense
3. Conjugate 15 key irregular verbs: *aller, faire, prendre, vouloir, pouvoir, savoir, venir, voir, mettre, dire, écrire, lire, partir, sortir, boire*

6. Form basic yes/no questions using intonation, *est-ce que*, and inversion
7. Recognize the difference between present tense in French and English progressive (*Je mange* = I eat / I am eating)

## Theory

### être (to be) — The Most Essential French Verb

*Être* is the most irregular and most important verb in French. It serves as a linking verb (*Je suis médecin* — I am a doctor), an auxiliary for compound tenses (*Je suis allé* — I went), and appears in countless fixed expressions.

```text
Present tense of être:
je suis       /ʒə sɥi/       I am
tu es         /ty ɛ/          you are (informal)
il/elle est   /il ɛ/          he/she is
nous sommes   /nu sɔm/        we are
vous êtes     /vuz ɛt/        you are (formal/plural)
ils/elles sont /il sɔ̃/        they are
```

**Uses of être:**
- Descriptions and identities: *Je suis étudiant(e), Elle est française*
- Locations (with the construction *se trouver* or directly): *Nous sommes à Paris*
- Dates and time: *Nous sommes lundi, Il est trois heures*
- Passive voice constructions (Module 09)
- Auxiliary for compound tenses with motion/state verbs (Module 06)

### avoir (to have) — The Second Essential Verb

*Avoir* is the most common auxiliary verb for compound tenses (*J'ai mangé* — I ate/have eaten) and also a standalone verb with many idiomatic uses.

```text
Present tense of avoir:
j'ai          /ʒe/            I have
tu as         /ty a/          you have
il/elle a     /il a/          he/she has
nous avons    /nuz avɔ̃/      we have
vous avez     /vuz ave/       you have
ils/elles ont /ilz ɔ̃/        they have
```

**Idiomatic uses of avoir (where French uses avoir, English uses "to be"):**
```text
avoir [number] ans           to be [X] years old
avoir faim / soif            to be hungry / thirsty
avoir chaud / froid          to be hot / cold
avoir peur (de)              to be afraid (of)
avoir besoin (de)            to need / to have need of
avoir envie (de)             to want / to feel like
avoir raison / tort          to be right / wrong
avoir l'air [adj]            to look / seem [adj]
```

### Regular -ER Verbs (the First and Largest Group)

Over 90% of French verbs are -ER verbs. The conjugation pattern is highly regular once you drop the -er infinitive ending and add the endings: **-e, -es, -e, -ons, -ez, -ent**.

```text
parler (to speak):
je parle      /ʒə paʁl/
tu parles     /ty paʁl/
il parle      /il paʁl/
nous parlons  /nu paʁlɔ̃/
vous parlez   /vu paʁle/
ils parlent   /il paʁl/
```

Note: **je, tu, il, and ils all sound identical** for -ER verbs: the -e, -es, -e, -ent endings are all silent. Only *nous* (-ons) and *vous* (-ez) are phonetically distinct.

**Common regular -ER verbs:**
*parler* (speak), *manger* (eat), *aimer* (love/like), *écouter* (listen), *regarder* (watch), *travailler* (work), *habiter* (live/reside), *chercher* (look for), *porter* (carry/wear), *jouer* (play), *penser* (think), *rester* (stay), *arriver* (arrive), *entrer* (enter), *donner* (give), *montrer* (show)

**Spelling changes in some -ER verbs:**
- Verbs ending in *-ger*: add *-e* before *-ons* ending: *nous mangeons* (not *mangons*)
- Verbs ending in *-cer*: change *c → ç* before *-ons*: *nous commençons*
- Verbs ending in *-yer*: change *y → i* before silent endings: *nettoyer → je nettoie*
- Verbs with *e* + consonant + *-er* (lever, acheter): double the consonant or add accent: *je lève, j'achète*

### Regular -IR Verbs (the Second Group)

-IR verbs add an *-iss-* infix for nous/vous/ils:

```text
finir (to finish):
je finis      /ʒə fini/
tu finis      /ty fini/
il finit      /il fini/
nous finissons /nu finisɔ̃/
vous finissez  /vu finise/
ils finissent  /il finis/
```

**Common regular -IR verbs:** *finir* (finish), *choisir* (choose), *réussir* (succeed), *réfléchir* (reflect/think), *grandir* (grow up), *obéir* (obey), *rougir* (blush), *maigrir* (lose weight)

### Regular -RE Verbs (the Third Group)

```text
vendre (to sell):
je vends      /ʒə vɑ̃/
tu vends      /ty vɑ̃/
il vend       /il vɑ̃/  (no ending added)
nous vendons  /nu vɑ̃dɔ̃/
vous vendez   /vu vɑ̃de/
ils vendent   /il vɑ̃d/
```

**Common -RE verbs:** *vendre* (sell), *attendre* (wait for), *entendre* (hear), *répondre* (answer), *perdre* (lose), *rendre* (give back/make), *descendre* (go down), *confondre* (confuse)

### Key Irregular Verbs

These 15 irregular verbs are used constantly in everyday French. They must be memorized:

**aller** (to go):
*je vais, tu vas, il va, nous allons, vous allez, ils vont*

**faire** (to do/make):
*je fais, tu fais, il fait, nous faisons, vous faites, ils font*

**prendre** (to take):
*je prends, tu prends, il prend, nous prenons, vous prenez, ils prennent*

**vouloir** (to want):
*je veux, tu veux, il veut, nous voulons, vous voulez, ils veulent*

**pouvoir** (can/to be able to):
*je peux, tu peux, il peut, nous pouvons, vous pouvez, ils peuvent*

**savoir** (to know — facts/how to):
*je sais, tu sais, il sait, nous savons, vous savez, ils savent*

**venir** (to come):
*je viens, tu viens, il vient, nous venons, vous venez, ils viennent*

**voir** (to see):
*je vois, tu vois, il voit, nous voyons, vous voyez, ils voient*

**mettre** (to put/put on):
*je mets, tu mets, il met, nous mettons, vous mettez, ils mettent*

**dire** (to say/tell):
*je dis, tu dis, il dit, nous disons, vous dites, ils disent*

**écrire** (to write):
*j'écris, tu écris, il écrit, nous écrivons, vous écrivez, ils écrivent*

**lire** (to read):
*je lis, tu lis, il lit, nous lisons, vous lisez, ils lisent*

**partir** (to leave/depart):
*je pars, tu pars, il part, nous partons, vous partez, ils partent*

**sortir** (to go out):
*je sors, tu sors, il sort, nous sortons, vous sortez, ils sortent*

**boire** (to drink):
*je bois, tu bois, il boit, nous buvons, vous buvez, ils boivent*

### Negation: ne...pas

Negation wraps around the conjugated verb:

```text
Structure: [Subject] + ne + [conjugated verb] + pas + [rest]

Je parle français.        → Je ne parle pas français.
Il a une voiture.         → Il n'a pas de voiture. (article also changes)
Nous allons à Paris.      → Nous n'allons pas à Paris.
```

Note: *ne* contracts to *n'* before a vowel.

### Questions

Three ways to ask a yes/no question:

```text
1. Intonation (most casual): Tu parles français ?
2. Est-ce que (standard): Est-ce que tu parles français ?
3. Inversion (formal): Parles-tu français ?
```

For inversion with *il/elle*, add *-t-* between verb and pronoun if the verb ends in a vowel:
```text
Il parle → Parle-t-il ? (not *Parle-il*)
Elle va → Va-t-elle ?
```

## Key Concepts

**The present tense covers "I do," "I am doing," and "I do do."** There is no progressive tense in French — *Je mange* covers all three English constructions. For an ongoing action right now, you can use *être en train de + infinitive*: *Je suis en train de manger* (I am [in the process of] eating) — but the simple present is usually sufficient.

**Regular -ER verb conjugation: most forms sound the same.** The je/tu/il/ils forms all sound identical — only spelling differs. This makes listening comprehension harder but speaking easier.

**savoir vs. connaître:** Both mean "to know" but differently. *Savoir* is for knowing facts, how to do things (*Je sais que..., Je sais nager*). *Connaître* is for knowing people and places (*Je connais Paris, Je connais Marie*).

## Examples

### Example 1: Using Avoir and Être Together

```text
Il a vingt-cinq ans et il est médecin.
He is 25 years old and he is a doctor.
(avoir for age; être for profession)

Elle a faim mais elle n'a pas de pain.
She is hungry but she doesn't have any bread.
(avoir for hunger; negation changes du/de la to de)
```

### Example 2: A Paragraph Using Multiple Verb Types

```text
Je m'appelle Léa. Je travaille dans un café à Paris (regular -ER).
Je commence à sept heures du matin (spelling-change -ER).
Je sers du café et des croissants (regular -RE: servir is actually irregular).
Je finis à quinze heures (regular -IR).
Après le travail, je vois mes amis (irregular: voir).
Nous allons souvent au cinéma (irregular: aller).
Je veux apprendre l'espagnol (irregular: vouloir + infinitive).
```

## Common Pitfalls

**Pitfall 1: Adding extra sounds to silent -ER verb endings.**

```text
Wrong:   ils parlent  /il paʁ.lɛnt/  ← pronouncing -ent
Correct: ils parlent  /il paʁl/      ← -ent is completely silent in -ER verbs
```

**Pitfall 2: Using *savoir* for people and places.**

```text
Wrong:   Je sais Paris. / Je sais Marie.
Correct: Je connais Paris. / Je connais Marie.
(savoir is for facts and skills; connaître is for people and places)
```

**Pitfall 3: Forgetting inversion requires a hyphen and sometimes -t-.**

```text
Wrong:   Parle il français ? / Va elle à Paris ?
Correct: Parle-t-il français ? / Va-t-elle à Paris ?
```

## Summary

- *Être* and *avoir* are the two foundational verbs — memorize them completely
- Regular -ER verbs: drop -er, add -e/-es/-e/-ons/-ez/-ent; je/tu/il/ils are silent, only nous/vous are phonetically distinct
- Regular -IR verbs: add -iss- infix for nous/vous/ils; *finissons, finissez, finissent*
- Regular -RE verbs: drop -re, add -s/-s/nothing/-ons/-ez/-ent
- 15 key irregular verbs must be memorized: aller, faire, prendre, vouloir, pouvoir, savoir, venir, voir, mettre, dire, écrire, lire, partir, sortir, boire
- Negation: *ne + [verb] + pas*; article changes after negation (du/de la/des → de)
- Three question formations: intonation (casual), *est-ce que* (standard), inversion (formal)
- *Savoir* = to know facts/skills; *connaître* = to know people and places
