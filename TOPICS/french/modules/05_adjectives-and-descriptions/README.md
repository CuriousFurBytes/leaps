## Overview

An adjective without agreement is like a verb without conjugation — grammatically incomplete. In French, adjectives must match the gender and number of the noun they modify, and their position relative to the noun carries meaning. Module 05 teaches you to describe the world in French with accuracy: what people look like, what their personality is, how places feel, and how things compare.

This module also introduces the BANGS rule (Beauty, Age, Numbers, Goodness, Size) for adjectives that precede the noun — a mnemonic that captures the most important class of preceding adjectives, including those that change meaning based on position.

## Theory

### Adjective Agreement (Accord de l'adjectif)

All French adjectives must agree in **gender** (masculine/feminine) and **number** (singular/plural) with the noun they modify. The base form of most adjectives is masculine singular.

**Regular agreement patterns:**

```text
Pattern: Masculine → Feminine → Masc. Plural → Fem. Plural

grand   → grande  → grands   → grandes    (add -e; add -s)
petit   → petite  → petits   → petites
intelligent → intelligente → intelligents → intelligentes
```

**Special feminine patterns:**

```text
-eux → -euse:   heureux → heureuse (happy)
-if  → -ive:    actif → active (active)
-el  → -elle:   naturel → naturelle (natural)
-il  → -ille:   gentil → gentille (kind)
-en  → -enne:   ancien → ancienne (ancient/former)
-er  → -ère:    premier → première (first)
-eur → -euse:   menteur → menteuse (lying)
```

**Invariable adjectives:** Color adjectives derived from nouns never change: *des yeux marron* (brown eyes — marron never takes -s or -e), *une chemise orange* (an orange shirt — orange stays orange).

**Agreement with multiple nouns:** When an adjective modifies nouns of different genders, the masculine plural form is used (the "generic" masculine in traditional grammar, though this is contested in contemporary French): *un homme et une femme intelligents*.

### Adjective Placement

**Default position: AFTER the noun.** Most French adjectives — especially those describing color, shape, origin, and physical characteristics — follow the noun.

```text
une maison blanche    (a white house)
un film français      (a French film)
une femme intelligente (an intelligent woman)
```

**The BANGS Rule: before the noun.** These categories of adjectives typically precede the noun:

- **B**eauty: *beau/belle* (beautiful), *joli(e)* (pretty), *laid(e)* (ugly)
- **A**ge: *jeune* (young), *vieux/vieille* (old), *nouveau/nouvelle* (new)
- **N**umbers/Order: *premier* (first), *deuxième* (second), *dernier* (last), *prochain* (next)
- **G**oodness: *bon/bonne* (good), *mauvais(e)* (bad), *meilleur(e)* (better), *gentil/gentille* (kind)
- **S**ize: *grand(e)* (big/tall), *petit(e)* (small), *gros/grosse* (fat/big), *long/longue* (long), *court(e)* (short), *haut(e)* (high)

**Liaison with preceding adjectives:** BANGS adjectives make liaison with the following noun if it starts with a vowel: *un grand‿arbre* (a large tree — liaison -d becomes /t/), *un bel‿homme* (a handsome man — *beau* becomes *bel* before vowel).

**Special forms before vowels:** *beau → bel, nouveau → nouvel, vieux → vieil* (masculine singular before vowels):

```text
un beau jardin    BUT  un bel arbre
un nouveau livre  BUT  un nouvel ami
un vieux château  BUT  un vieil homme
```

**Adjectives that change meaning by position:**

```text
un grand homme     (a great man — famous/important)
un homme grand     (a tall man — physically tall)

un ancien élève    (a former student)
un bâtiment ancien (an old/ancient building)

un cher ami        (a dear friend)
un restaurant cher (an expensive restaurant)

un seul enfant     (an only child)
un enfant seul     (a lonely/alone child)

la propre voiture  (one's own car)
une voiture propre (a clean car)
```

### Comparatives and Superlatives

**Comparatives (more/less/as...as):**

```text
plus + adj + que     more ... than     Elle est plus grande que lui.
moins + adj + que    less ... than     Il est moins patient que moi.
aussi + adj + que    as ... as         Je suis aussi fatigué que toi.
```

**Superlatives (the most/least):**

```text
le/la/les plus + adj    the most ...    C'est la plus belle ville.
le/la/les moins + adj   the least ...   C'est le moins cher.
```

Note: The superlative article (*le/la/les*) must agree with the noun's gender.

**Irregular comparatives and superlatives:**

```text
bon(ne)    → meilleur(e)   (better)       → le/la meilleur(e) (the best)
mauvais(e) → pire          (worse)        → le/la pire (the worst)
bien       → mieux         (better, adv.) → le mieux (the best, adv.)
mal        → pis / plus mal (worse, adv.)
petit(e)   → moindre (lesser, in value) or plus petit(e) (physically smaller)
```

## Key Concepts

**Agreement is non-optional.** *Un maison blanc* is not "approximately correct" French — it is wrong in a way that every French speaker will notice. Agreement errors accumulate in compound sentences, where multiple adjectives must all agree with their respective nouns.

**The BANGS mnemonic captures the most common preceding adjectives but not all.** A few additional adjectives habitually precede the noun: *autre* (other), *même* (same), *tel* (such), *certain* (certain — meaning "a certain"), *quelque* (some). These must be learned individually.

**Position changes meaning for a specific set of adjectives.** The most important pairs are *grand homme/homme grand*, *ancien élève/bâtiment ancien*, *cher ami/restaurant cher*, *propre voiture/voiture propre*, *seul enfant/enfant seul*. Knowing these pairs demonstrates genuine grammatical sophistication.

## Examples

### Example 1: Describing a Person

```text
Mon ami Thomas est grand et mince. Il a les cheveux bruns et les yeux bleus.
Il est intelligent et travailleur, mais parfois un peu têtu.
C'est un bon ami — il est toujours gentil avec moi.

My friend Thomas is tall and slim. He has brown hair and blue eyes.
He is intelligent and hardworking, but sometimes a little stubborn.
He is a good friend — he is always kind to me.

Notes:
- grand, mince: follow the noun (physical descriptions)
- bon: precedes the noun (BANGS: goodness)
- gentil: irregular feminine would be gentille (here masc., agrees with ami)
```

### Example 2: Comparative Sentences

```text
Le métro est plus rapide que le bus, mais moins confortable.
The metro is faster than the bus, but less comfortable.

Cette boulangerie est la meilleure du quartier.
This bakery is the best in the neighborhood.
(meilleure: feminine, agrees with boulangerie)
```

## Common Pitfalls

**Pitfall 1: Invariable color adjectives.**

```text
Wrong:   des yeux marrons
Correct: des yeux marron    (marron, orange, etc. are invariable)
```

**Pitfall 2: Using *bon* where *bien* is needed (and vice versa).**

```text
bon/bonne: adjective (modifies noun) — un bon livre (a good book)
bien: adverb (modifies verb/adjective) — Il parle bien français.

Wrong:   Elle parle bon français.
Correct: Elle parle bien français. (bien modifies the verb parle)
```

**Pitfall 3: Forgetting the masculine-before-vowel special forms.**

```text
Wrong:   un beau homme, un nouveau ami
Correct: un bel homme, un nouvel ami
```

## Summary

- Adjectives must agree in gender and number with the noun they modify
- Regular feminine: add *-e*; regular plural: add *-s*; special patterns: -eux→-euse, -if→-ive, -el→-elle, -er→-ère
- Default position: after the noun; BANGS adjectives precede the noun
- Special masculine-before-vowel forms: *bel, nouvel, vieil*
- Some adjectives change meaning depending on position (grand, ancien, cher, propre, seul)
- Comparatives: *plus/moins/aussi + adj + que*
- Superlatives: *le/la/les plus/moins + adj*
- Irregular: *bon → meilleur, mauvais → pire, bien → mieux*
