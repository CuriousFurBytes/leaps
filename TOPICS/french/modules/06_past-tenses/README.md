## Overview

The choice between passé composé and imparfait is the central challenge of French past narrative — and one of the most conceptually demanding things an English speaker encounters when learning French. English uses "I ate," "I was eating," "I used to eat," and "I have eaten" with fairly predictable rules. French condenses and recombines these into two tenses that operate on different principles: **aspect** (how an action is framed — bounded vs. unbounded) rather than just chronological distance from the present.

The passé composé reports **completed, bounded events**: things that happened and finished. The imparfait paints the **background, states, and repeated or habitual actions** that provide context for those events. Together they work like a film: the imparfait is the scene setting, the passé composé is the action.

## Theory

### The Passé Composé: Structure and Auxiliary Choice

The passé composé (literally "composed past" — a compound tense) is formed with:

```text
Subject + auxiliary (être or avoir, présent) + past participle

J'ai mangé.          I ate / I have eaten.
Il est parti.        He left / He has left.
Nous avons fini.     We finished / We have finished.
```

**Regular past participles:**
```text
-ER verbs → -é:   parler → parlé,  manger → mangé,  aller → allé
-IR verbs → -i:   finir → fini,    partir → parti,   choisir → choisi
-RE verbs → -u:   vendre → vendu,  attendre → attendu,  répondre → répondu
```

**Auxiliary selection — the critical rule:**

**Most verbs use *avoir*.** The 17 motion/state verbs use *être*. All reflexive verbs use *être*.

The 17 être verbs (learned as pairs of opposites):
```text
aller / venir            (go / come)
arriver / partir         (arrive / leave)
entrer / sortir          (enter / exit)
monter / descendre       (go up / go down)
naître / mourir          (be born / die)
rester                   (stay)
tomber                   (fall)
retourner                (go back)
passer (when intransitive) (pass by)
devenir, revenir, parvenir (compound forms of venir)
```

Memory aid: DR & MRS P. VANDERTRAMP (Descendre, Rester, Monter, Revenir, Sortir, Passer, Venir, Arriver, Naître, Devenir, Entrer, Rentrer, Tomber, Retourner, Aller, Mourir, Partir)

> [!WARNING]
> Some verbs can take either auxiliary depending on usage. *Monter, descendre, sortir, rentrer, passer* use *être* when intransitive (no direct object), but *avoir* when transitive (with a direct object): *Je suis sorti* (I went out — intransitive) vs. *J'ai sorti le chien* (I took out the dog — transitive with direct object).

### Past Participle Agreement Rules

**With *être* auxiliary:** The past participle agrees with the subject like an adjective.
```text
Il est parti.     (masc. sing. — no change)
Elle est partie.  (fem. sing. — add -e)
Ils sont partis.  (masc. pl. — add -s)
Elles sont parties. (fem. pl. — add -es)
```

**With *avoir* auxiliary:** The past participle normally does NOT agree. Exception: it agrees with a **preceding direct object** (a direct object that comes before the verb — typically a pronoun or in a relative clause).
```text
J'ai mangé les pommes.        (no agreement — direct object follows verb)
Les pommes que j'ai mangées.  (agreement — the pommes precedes via relative clause)
Je les ai mangées.            (agreement — les = les pommes, precedes verb)
```

This rule is important for C1-level writing. At A2–B1 level, the key takeaway is: with *avoir*, the past participle usually does not change.

### The Imparfait: Formation and Usage

**Formation:** Take the *nous* form of the present tense → remove *-ons* → add imparfait endings.

Endings: **-ais, -ais, -ait, -ions, -iez, -aient**

```text
parler (nous parlons → stem: parl-):
je parlais, tu parlais, il parlait, nous parlions, vous parliez, ils parlaient

avoir (nous avons → stem: av-):
j'avais, tu avais, il avait, nous avions, vous aviez, ils avaient

Exception: être has irregular stem ét-:
j'étais, tu étais, il était, nous étions, vous étiez, ils étaient
```

Note: *nous* and *vous* imparfait forms often look like present *faire* conjugations with spelling changes for *-ger* and *-cer* verbs. Only *être* has a completely irregular stem.

**Usage of the imparfait:**

1. **Descriptions and states** in the past (how things were, not what happened):
   ```text
   Il faisait beau. The weather was nice.
   Elle était fatiguée. She was tired.
   J'avais faim. I was hungry.
   ```

2. **Habitual or repeated actions** in the past (used to, would):
   ```text
   Quand j'étais enfant, je jouais au foot tous les jours.
   When I was a child, I played football every day.
   ```

3. **Ongoing action** interrupted by a passé composé event:
   ```text
   Je dormais quand le téléphone a sonné.
   I was sleeping when the telephone rang.
   ```

4. **Indirect speech** after reporting verbs in the past:
   ```text
   Il a dit qu'il était fatigué.
   He said he was tired. (était = imparfait after reporting verb)
   ```

### The PC/Imparfait Distinction: Aspect, Not Time

The key to understanding the distinction is **aspect** — the "shape" of an event in time:

- **Passé composé** = bounded/completed: the action is presented as having a definite beginning and end
- **Imparfait** = unbounded/ongoing: the action is presented as background, ongoing, or habitual — no defined endpoint

```text
Hier, j'ai mangé une pizza.           (PC: completed event — I ate a pizza [and finished it])
Hier soir, je mangeais une pizza...   (Imparfait: ongoing/background — I was eating a pizza [when something happened])

Quand j'étais enfant, je mangeais des pizzas tous les vendredis.
(Imparfait: habitual past — I used to eat pizzas every Friday)
```

**Signal words for passé composé:** *hier* (yesterday), *soudain* (suddenly), *tout à coup* (all of a sudden), *d'abord...puis...ensuite...enfin* (first...then...then...finally), *une fois* (once), *ce matin-là* (that morning)

**Signal words for imparfait:** *autrefois* (in the past), *d'habitude* (usually), *chaque jour/semaine* (every day/week), *souvent* (often), *toujours* (always), *quand j'étais enfant* (when I was a child), *à cette époque* (at that time)

### Verbs That Change Meaning in Past Tenses

These verbs have distinct meanings depending on which past tense is used:

| Verb | Passé composé | Imparfait |
|------|--------------|-----------|
| *savoir* | found out, realized | knew (was aware of) |
| *connaître* | met (for the first time) | knew (was acquainted with) |
| *pouvoir* | managed to, succeeded in | was able to (in general) |
| *vouloir* | decided to, tried to | wanted (ongoing desire) |

```text
J'ai su la vérité. (I found out the truth.)
Je savais la vérité. (I knew the truth — all along.)

Il a pu ouvrir la porte. (He managed to open the door.)
Il pouvait ouvrir la porte. (He was able to open the door — generally capable.)
```

## Key Concepts

**The passé composé is the main narrative tense.** In spoken French, the passé composé has almost completely replaced the passé simple (a literary past tense used in formal writing) for reporting past events. At A2–C1 level, the passé composé is your everyday past tense for completed actions.

**The imparfait is the "backdrop" tense.** Think of a film: the imparfait is the establishing shot (the weather, the setting, the habitual situation); the passé composé is the action sequence (what happened, what changed).

**Past participle agreement with être is consistent and audible.** With être verbs, the agreement is both written and spoken: *elle est partie* has an audible -e that *il est parti* does not.

## Examples

### Example 1: Combining Both Tenses in a Narrative

```text
Hier soir, je regardais la télévision quand mon ami a téléphoné.
Il était tard — presque minuit — mais j'ai répondu immédiatement.
Il m'a dit qu'il avait un problème. Je suis sorti et nous avons
parlé pendant deux heures dans la rue. Il faisait froid, mais
nous n'avons pas arrêté de parler.

Yesterday evening, I was watching television when my friend called.
It was late — almost midnight — but I answered immediately.
He told me he had a problem. I went out and we talked for two hours
in the street. It was cold, but we didn't stop talking.

Notes:
- regardais: imparfait (ongoing background action)
- a téléphoné: PC (event that interrupted the background)
- était: imparfait (description of state — how late it was)
- ai répondu: PC (completed action)
- a dit: PC (completed speech act)
- avait: imparfait (reported state — within indirect speech)
- suis sorti: PC (completed motion with être auxiliary)
- avons parlé: PC (completed action)
- faisait: imparfait (description — the cold)
- n'avons pas arrêté: PC (completed non-stopping)
```

## Common Pitfalls

**Pitfall 1: Using passé composé for habitual actions.**

```text
Wrong:   Quand j'étais enfant, j'ai joué au foot chaque jour.
Correct: Quand j'étais enfant, je jouais au foot chaque jour.
(Habitual action → imparfait)
```

**Pitfall 2: Forgetting être auxiliary verbs.**

```text
Wrong:   Je suis arrivé et j'ai sorti de la voiture.
Correct: Je suis arrivé et je suis sorti de la voiture.
(Both aller/arriver and sortir take être)
```

**Pitfall 3: Misapplying past participle agreement.**

```text
Wrong (with être): Il est arrivée. (il = masculine, no -e agreement)
Correct: Il est arrivé. (masculine singular — base form)

Wrong (with avoir): J'ai mangé*es* les pommes.
Correct: J'ai mangé les pommes. (no agreement when object follows)
Exception: Les pommes que j'ai mangées. (agreement with preceding object)
```

## Summary

- Passé composé = auxiliary (avoir or être, présent) + past participle; used for completed, bounded past actions
- 17 verbs (and all reflexives) use être; everything else uses avoir
- Past participle agreement: with être, agree with subject; with avoir, agree with preceding direct object only
- Imparfait = nous-stem + endings (-ais, -ais, -ait, -ions, -iez, -aient); irregular: être → ét-
- Imparfait for descriptions, states, habitual/repeated actions, and background ongoing action
- The key distinction is aspect: passé composé = bounded event; imparfait = ongoing/habitual background
- Signal words: soudain/hier/d'abord (PC) vs. d'habitude/autrefois/chaque jour (Imparfait)
- Some verbs (savoir, connaître, pouvoir, vouloir) change meaning depending on which tense is used
