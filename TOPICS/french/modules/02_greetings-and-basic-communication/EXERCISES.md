# Exercises: Module 02 — Greetings and Basic Communication

## Instructions

Complete each exercise in order. Exercises increase in difficulty. Write your answers in French without consulting notes first, then verify.

---

## Easy Exercises (1–3)

### Exercise 1

**Difficulty:** Easy
**Objective:** Practice numbers 70–100.

Write out the following numbers in French words. Pay special attention to 70–99.

```text
71, 75, 80, 81, 84, 90, 91, 95, 99, 100
```

---

### Exercise 2

**Difficulty:** Easy
**Objective:** Days, months, and dates.

Write the following dates in full French sentences using the format *C'est le [date] [month].*

```text
March 15, July 4, January 1, December 25, November 11
```

---

### Exercise 3

**Difficulty:** Easy
**Objective:** Tell the time.

Write the time as a full French sentence (*Il est...*) for each of the following:

```text
9:00am, 12:00pm, 3:30pm, 7:45pm, 11:15am, 8:50am
```

---

## Medium Exercises (4–6)

### Exercise 4

**Difficulty:** Medium
**Objective:** Write a self-introduction.

Write a 5-sentence introduction in French as if you were introducing yourself to a French class. Include: your name, your nationality (with correct gender), where you are from, where you live now, and one thing about yourself (job/study/hobby).

---

### Exercise 5

**Difficulty:** Medium
**Objective:** Nationalities and gender agreement.

Rewrite each sentence, changing the subject from masculine to feminine (or vice versa), and adjusting the nationality adjective accordingly.

```text
1. Thomas est américain.       → Marie ___
2. Elle est japonaise.         → Il ___
3. Marc est brésilien.         → Sofia ___
4. Anna est allemande.         → Klaus ___
5. Il est espagnol.            → Elle ___
```

---

### Exercise 6

**Difficulty:** Medium
**Objective:** Register — formal vs. informal.

Rewrite the following informal sentences in formal French. Change every element that is register-specific (pronouns, verb forms, vocabulary).

```text
1. T'as l'heure ?
2. Tu viens d'où ?
3. Salut, comment tu t'appelles ?
4. Ouais, ça va. Et toi ?
5. À plus !
```

---

## Hard Exercises (7–8)

### Exercise 7

**Difficulty:** Hard
**Objective:** Write and analyze a formal introduction dialogue.

Write a 12-line dialogue (6 lines each) between two people meeting formally at a business conference. One is French, the other is from a country of your choice. The dialogue must:
- Use *vous* throughout
- Include an exchange of names, nationalities, and professions
- Include a mention of a date or time (e.g., when the next meeting is)
- Use at least 3 of the essential conversation phrases from the module
- End with an appropriate formal farewell

After writing the dialogue, note which register markers you used and why each was appropriate.

---

### Exercise 8

**Difficulty:** Hard
**Objective:** Correct errors in a piece of student writing.

The following text was written by a student. It contains 8 errors (capitalization, number spelling, date format, register mixing, and agreement errors). Find all 8 errors, explain each one, and rewrite the corrected version.

```text
Je suis Américain. Je m'appelle David et j'ai trente-et-un ans.
Aujourd'hui c'est le un Novembre. Mon anniversaire est le
quatre-vingts-neuf décembre — ah non, je veux dire le vingt-neuf décembre.
Je parle Anglais et un peu de Français. À bientôt, salut !
```

## Expert Exercise (9)

### Exercise 9

**Difficulty:** Expert
**Objective:** Cultural and linguistic analysis of French number systems.

The French vigesimal counting system (60 = soixante, 70 = soixante-dix, 80 = quatre-vingts, 90 = quatre-vingt-dix) is unique among major European languages. Answer the following:

1. What is a "vigesimal" counting system? Name one other culture or language that uses base-20 counting.
2. How does Belgian/Swiss French solve this complexity (septante, huitante/octante, nonante)? Why are these forms not used in France despite being more logical?
3. Write a realistic scenario where the 70–99 number system could cause a genuine communication error (e.g., misheard prices, ages, addresses) and explain how context prevents or causes misunderstanding.
4. French ordinal numbers (first, second, third) are formed differently from English: *premier, deuxième/second, troisième*. Write the ordinals from 1st to 10th in French, noting the two options for "second/deuxième" and when each is preferred.
