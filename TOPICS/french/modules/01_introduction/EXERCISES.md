```text
Dialogue with errors (errors marked with *):

A: Vous êtes de Pari*-ss*?
```

## Expert Exercise (9)

### Exercise 9

**Difficulty:** Expert
**Objective:** Synthesize phonological knowledge to explain French music/poetry.

French poetry and song have a special relationship with liaison and elision — features that are mandatory in speech become subject to artistic choice in verse. Read the following excerpt from a French children's song (*Frère Jacques*) and answer the questions.

```text
Frère Jacques, Frère Jacques,
Dormez-vous? Dormez-vous?
Sonnez les matines! Sonnez les matines!
Din, din, don. Din, din, don.
```

**Questions:**

1. In the line *Dormez-vous?*, identify the type of construction (inversion question). Where does the liaison occur? Write the phonetic form of *Dormez-vous*.

2. In *Sonnez les matines*, identify which word pair could create a liaison, whether it is obligatory/optional/forbidden, and what the liaison consonant sound would be.

3. The word *Frère* contains an accent mark. Name it, explain its phonological function, and write the IPA of *Frère*.

4. Compare the pronunciation rules in French song vs. French speech. In formal sung French, elision is sometimes suppressed for musical reasons (a syllable that would be elided in speech is instead pronounced to fill a musical beat). How would singing *Frère Jacques* syllable-by-syllable help a language learner — and what risk does it carry?

5. Write a 4-line simple verse in French (you may invent nonsense content using Module 01 vocabulary) that demonstrates at least one obligatory liaison, one elision, and one nasal vowel. Mark each phenomenon.
