## Overview

Fundos Imobiliários (FIIs) are the most popular passive income vehicle among Brazilian retail investors — and for good reason. They combine the income generation of real estate (monthly rent) with the accessibility and liquidity of stock exchange trading. An investor with R$ 100 can buy a fraction of a shopping center, a logistics warehouse, or a hospital building, and receive a proportional share of the rent collected.

By law, Brazilian FIIs must distribute at least 95% of their semi-annual profits as dividends to quotaholders. In practice, most distribute monthly. And crucially, these dividends are **income-tax-exempt** for individual investors (subject to standard qualification rules). In a country where most investment income is taxed at 15–22.5%, this exemption is a significant structural advantage.

Brazil has over 450 FIIs listed on B3 with a combined market cap exceeding R$ 150 billion. The diversity spans office buildings, shopping malls, logistics parks, hospitals, schools, data centers, CRI (real estate receivables) portfolios, and fund-of-fund structures. Building a diversified FII portfolio is one of the most practical paths to generating passive income in Brazil.

### FII Types

**FIIs de Tijolo (Brick FIIs)** — Own and operate physical real estate assets. Rent from tenants flows to quotaholders as dividends. Subtypes:

| Segment | Examples | Risk Profile |
|---------|----------|-------------|
| Lajes corporativas (office) | CSHG Real Estate (HGRE11) | Moderate; vacancy risk, WFH impact |
| Galpões logísticos | Bresco Logística (BRCO11) | Lower; e-commerce demand driver |
| Shoppings centers | XPML11, VISC11 | Moderate; consumer spending dependent |
| Hospitalar | HCTR11 | Lower; long-term contracts typical |
| Agências bancárias | BBFI11 | Lower; single tenant risk |

**FIIs de Papel (Paper FIIs)** — Invest in real estate debt instruments (CRI — Certificados de Recebíveis Imobiliários) rather than physical properties. Returns are more like fixed income but with better tax treatment (IR-exempt dividends).

**Fundos de Fundos (FoF FIIs)** — Invest in portfolios of other FIIs. Provide built-in diversification. Higher tax efficiency but layered management fees.

### Key KPIs

**Dividend Yield (DY):** `DY = (Últimos 12 meses de dividendos por cota) / (Preço de cota atual)`
A sustainable DY of 8–12% annually is typical for Brazilian FIIs. Very high DY (>15%) can signal dividend cuts ahead.

**P/VP (Preço/Valor Patrimonial):** `P/VP = Preço de mercado / Valor patrimonial por cota`
P/VP < 1.0: cotas trading below book value — potential buy signal.
P/VP > 1.0: cotas trading above book value — requires strong earnings justification.

**Vacância Física:** Percentage of GLA (gross leasable area) not currently rented. Lower = better.
**Vacância Financeira:** Percentage of potential rental income not being collected. Includes rent-free periods, discounts, and physically vacant space.

**Taxa de Administração:** Annual management fee as % of assets. Typically 0.5–1.5% for tijolo FIIs, up to 2% for FoF. Higher fees compress investor returns.

### Dividend Tax Exemption Rules

FII dividends are IR-exempt for individual investors provided ALL of the following conditions are met:
1. The FII has at least 50 quotaholders (cotistas)
2. No single quotaholder holds more than 10% of total quotas
3. The FII is listed on B3 and has at least 30% of its quotas traded in the last 12 months

Most listed FIIs meet these conditions. However, capital gains on FII quota sales are always taxable at 20% (no isenção — this is different from ações).

## Key Concepts

**Cota** — A FII "share" — the unit of ownership. FIIs issue cotas traded on B3. Price typically between R$ 10–200 per cota.

**GLA (Gross Leasable Area)** — Total area of physical real estate available for rent in a FII's portfolio. Used to measure scale and calculate per-GLA metrics.

**CRI (Certificado de Recebíveis Imobiliários)** — Real estate receivable certificates. Debt instruments backed by property mortgages or rents. The core asset of papel FIIs.

**Relatório Gerencial** — Monthly or quarterly management report published by the FII's administrator. Contains vacancy rates, rental income, expenses, dividends declared, and portfolio news. Essential reading for serious FII investors.

**Vacância** — Vacancy. The percentage of property not generating income. Physical vacância ≠ financial vacância (tenants with rent-free periods count as physically occupied but financially vacant).

### FII screening example: identifying a candidate

Investor criteria: DY > 9%, P/VP < 1.1, vacância < 10%, taxa de administração < 1.5%, segment = galpões logísticos.

Using Investidor10 or Funds Explorer, filter the FII universe:
- Of ~450 FIIs, approximately 30–40 galpão FIIs exist
- Applying DY > 9% reduces to ~15–20
- Applying P/VP < 1.1 reduces to ~8–12
- Applying vacância < 10% reduces to ~5–8

Then read the relatório gerencial of each remaining candidate to assess lease duration, tenant quality, and pipeline.

## Common Pitfalls

- **Chasing the highest DY** — A FII paying 18% DY may be distributing amortization of capital (devolução de capital), not true income. Always check if dividends exceed net income in the relatório gerencial.
- **Ignoring vacância trends** — Vacância rising quarter over quarter is a leading indicator of future dividend cuts.
- **Concentration in one segment** — A portfolio of 10 shopping FIIs is not diversified. Diversify across tijolo segments AND include some papel FIIs.
- **Forgetting the 20% GCAP on sales** — FII quota gains are taxed at 20% with no isenção. Many investors discover this only when filing their IRPF.
- **Ignoring the relatório gerencial** — Numbers on a screener are snapshots. The management report tells you why, and what's coming.

## Summary

- FIIs are Brazil's most accessible passive income vehicle: traded on B3 from R$ 10/cota, paying monthly tax-free dividends
- Three main types: tijolo (physical properties), papel (CRI debt instruments), and fundo de fundos (FoF)
- Key KPIs: DY, P/VP, vacância física/financeira, taxa de administração — all must be evaluated together, not in isolation
- Dividends are IR-exempt if the FII has ≥50 quotaholders and no single investor holds >10% — capital gains on cota sales are always taxed at 20%
- Diversification across segments (office, logistics, shopping, paper) reduces single-segment risk
- The relatório gerencial is the most important document for ongoing FII analysis — more informative than any screener
