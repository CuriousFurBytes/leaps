## Essential Books

### Food Science and Culinary Theory

1. **"On Food and Cooking: The Science and Lore of the Kitchen" by Harold McGee** (revised edition
   2004, Scribner) — The definitive reference for the food science underpinning every culinary
   technique. Covers the chemistry of proteins, fats, carbohydrates, dairy, eggs, bread, and
   sauces in exhaustive detail. Essential reading for Modules 03–08.
   [Verify: confirm current publisher and ISBN before sharing with learners]

2. **"The Food Lab: Better Home Cooking Through Science" by J. Kenji López-Alt** (2015, W. W.
   Norton & Company) — Applies rigorous scientific testing to home cooking questions: why does
   rest time matter for steak, what fat percentage makes the best burger, how do you achieve a
   perfect hollandaise. Highly practical complement to the theory in Modules 03–05.
   [Verify: confirm publication details]

3. **"Le Guide Culinaire" by Auguste Escoffier** (1903; English translation available from Wiley)
   — The foundational classical French reference. Not a learner-friendly read, but essential as
   a professional reference once you have completed Modules 01–04.
   [Verify: confirm current English translation publisher]

### Patisserie and Baking

4. **"The Professional Pastry Chef" by Bo Friberg** — Comprehensive professional-level patisserie
   reference covering pastry doughs, chocolates, confections, and plated desserts. Relevant from
   Module 07 onwards.
   [Verify: confirm current edition and publisher]

5. **"Bread Science" by Emily Buehler** — Clear, accessible explanation of fermentation, gluten
   development, and the chemistry of bread baking. Strong complement to Module 06.
   [Verify: confirm author and title]

## Reference Books

6. **"Larousse Gastronomique"** (various editions, Larousse) — The French culinary encyclopedia.
   Invaluable for looking up French culinary terms, classical recipes, and the historical origins
   of dishes and techniques. Use alongside the GLOSSARY.md in this topic.

7. **"The Flavor Bible" by Karen Page and Andrew Dornenburg** (2008, Little, Brown) — A
   cross-reference of ingredient flavor affinities, organized alphabetically. Excellent for
   Module 11 (Menu Planning) when building flavor pairings.
   [Verify: confirm publication details]

## Online Resources

8. **Serious Eats (seriouseats.com)** — Editorial food site with rigorous recipe testing and
   food-science explanations. Kenji López-Alt and other contributors publish detailed technique
   articles that complement every module in this topic.

9. **Le Cordon Bleu Technique Videos** — Le Cordon Bleu publishes free demonstration videos on
   their YouTube channel covering classical French techniques from knife skills to pastry.
