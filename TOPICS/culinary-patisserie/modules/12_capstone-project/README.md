## Overview

This is the capstone project for the Culinary Arts and Patisserie topic. It is not a lecture
or a test — it is a real cooking project that requires you to synthesise everything you have
learned across all twelve modules and produce a complete, documented, multi-course meal.

The output is not just the food. It is a portfolio document: a written menu card, standard
recipe cards for each course, food cost calculations, and photographs of every course plated.
This is what a working chef produces when designing a new menu, and it is the measure of
genuine professional competence.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Do not look for a complete recipe solution here.** This module provides scaffolding,
> milestones, and help checkpoints to guide you through the project, but the design decisions
> are yours. A capstone project that is copy-pasted from a source is not a capstone — it is
> a missed opportunity to build real skill. Use the Getting Unstuck section if you are stuck;
> do not skip the work.

## Project Brief

### The Menu

Design and execute a 4-course tasting menu:

| Course | French Term | Description | Constraints |
|--------|------------|-------------|-------------|
| **Course 1** | *Amuse-bouche* | One-to-two bites; a single, conceptually bold flavour statement | Must be served at room temperature or chilled; no hot components |
| **Course 2** | *Entrée* | First course; light; establishes the menu's flavour direction | Must include a sauce derived from a mother sauce or stock |
| **Course 3** | *Plat principal* | Main course; the emotional and flavour peak of the meal | Must demonstrate at least one dry-heat and one moist-heat method; must include a plated vegetable component |
| **Course 4** | *Dessert* | Sweet conclusion; must apply at least one patisserie technique | Must use at least one technique from Modules 07, 08, or 09 |

### Design Requirements

- The four courses must form a coherent progression: light → building → peak → resolution
- At least three of the four courses must include a sauce or coulis
- At least one course must demonstrate knife work (brunoise, julienne, or tourné as a garnish)
- The dessert must include a textural contrast element (crisp, crumb, or tuile)

### Documentation Requirements

Submit the following for each course:
1. **Standard recipe card** — ingredient list with weights; full procedure; allergen information
2. **Food cost calculation** — ingredient costs and food cost percentage
3. **Plating diagram** — a sketch or photograph of the intended plate layout
4. **Final photograph** — one photograph of the plated course

### The Menu Card

Write a printed or typed menu card in the style of a restaurant:
- Course names and brief descriptions (one to two lines each)
- Wine pairing suggestion (optional; may be non-alcoholic pairing)
- A title for the menu (e.g., "A Winter Menu" or "Six Weeks of Learning: A Tasting Menu")

## Milestones

Complete the milestones in order. Each milestone is a checkpoint — verify your work before
proceeding to the next.

### Milestone 1: Menu Concept (Before any cooking)

- [ ] Write the full menu concept in one paragraph: what is the flavour story of the menu? What season, region, or ingredient theme connects the courses?
- [ ] List all four courses with working names
- [ ] Identify which module techniques will appear in each course (e.g., "Entrée: velouté-derived sauce from Module 03; brunoise garnish from Module 02")
- [ ] Complete a shopping list for all four courses

---

### Milestone 2: Standard Recipe Cards (Before cooking)

- [ ] Write a complete standard recipe card for each of the four courses
- [ ] Calculate food cost for each course using the yield percentage method from Module 11
- [ ] Sketch a plating diagram for each course

> [!TIP]
> If you are stuck on recipe design, review the examples in Modules 03–09. Use the CHEATSHEET
> for ratio references. The recipe card does not need to be an original recipe — a classical
> preparation executed correctly is entirely appropriate.

---

### Milestone 3: Test-Cook Each Course Separately

- [ ] Cook Course 1 (amuse-bouche) as a standalone test; taste, assess, adjust recipe card
- [ ] Cook Course 2 (entrée) including the sauce; assess sauce consistency, seasoning, plating
- [ ] Cook Course 3 (plat principal); verify correct internal temperature on protein; rest correctly; plate
- [ ] Cook Course 4 (dessert); verify texture; practice the plating before the final service

> [!TIP]
> Test-cooking separately is not optional. Finding a problem in a test cook is far less stressful
> than finding it on the day of service. Write notes on each test cook directly in your recipe cards.

---

### Milestone 4: Full Service Rehearsal

- [ ] Cook all four courses in sequence for at least two diners (family, friends, or yourself with documentation)
- [ ] Time the sequence: courses should arrive at 15–20 minute intervals; plan your timing backwards from the desired service time using the brigade system approach from Module 01
- [ ] Photograph every plated course during service

---

### Milestone 5: Documentation Package

- [ ] Compile final recipe cards (updated from test cook notes)
- [ ] Write a one-page reflection: what did you learn? What would you change? Which module techniques felt most natural? Which were most challenging?
- [ ] Assemble the final menu card, recipe cards, photographs, and reflection into a single document

## Getting Unstuck

<details>
<summary>Help: I don't know how to design an amuse-bouche</summary>

An amuse-bouche is a gift from the kitchen — one or two bites that signal the style and
character of the meal to come. It should be:
- Complete: a balanced bite with all the elements of a full dish in miniature
- Conceptually clear: it makes one flavour point, not several
- Easy to eat standing or with a spoon

**Ideas to get started:**
- A small spoon of cold cucumber soup with a dot of yoghurt
- A blini with crème fraîche and a small amount of smoked fish
- A tiny tartlet of caramelised onion and blue cheese
- A cold asparagus spear wrapped in prosciutto with a shaving of parmesan

Return to Module 01 (flavour balance) and Module 09 (plating scale) for design principles.
</details>

<details>
<summary>Help: My sauce from Module 03 is breaking or tasting flat</summary>

If your sauce is breaking: check that your Hollandaise is not too hot (above 65 °C) and that
you added butter slowly. For a velouté or béchamel derivative, check your roux — it may be
under-cooked (raw flour taste) or the ratio may be off.

If your sauce tastes flat: apply the flavour balance principles from Module 01. Add a small
amount of acid (lemon juice, white wine vinegar) and taste. Add salt in small pinches. A
final knob of cold butter swirled in (monter au beurre) will enrich and gloss any sauce.

Return to: [[culinary-patisserie/modules/03_stocks-and-sauces]], particularly the mother sauces
theory and common pitfalls.
</details>

<details>
<summary>Help: My protein is overcooked or undercooked</summary>

The cause is almost always one of three things:
1. **Uneven thickness** — pound or butterfly thick portions to even thickness before cooking
2. **No thermometer** — guessing doneness by touch is a skill that takes years to develop; use a thermometer for every protein until you have cooked it dozens of times
3. **Pan temperature wrong** — too high and the outside burns before the inside cooks; too low and you steam rather than sear

Safe internal temperatures are in the [CHEATSHEET.md](../../CHEATSHEET.md).

Return to: [[culinary-patisserie/modules/04_cooking-methods]].
</details>

<details>
<summary>Help: My dessert won't set / is the wrong texture</summary>

**Panna cotta won't set:** increase gelatin by 10% or check that your gelatin was fully bloomed
in cold water before dissolving; check that you did not boil the cream after adding gelatin
(boiling weakens gelatin's setting power).

**Mousse collapsed:** check that your whipped cream was at the correct stage (soft-to-medium
peaks) before folding; avoid over-folding (5–8 folds maximum for each addition of cream).

**Pastry shell shrank:** the dough was not rested long enough or was over-worked; review the
rubbing method in Module 07.

Return to: [[culinary-patisserie/modules/09_desserts-and-plating]] and [[culinary-patisserie/modules/07_pastry-fundamentals]].
</details>

<details>
<summary>Help: My plating looks amateur compared to what I imagined</summary>

Plating is a skill that improves with deliberate practice. Before plating the final dish:
1. **Sketch the plate** on paper first
2. **Work on a cold, clean plate** (warm plates cause sauces to run and gelatin to melt)
3. **Apply sauces first** (they are the canvas); proteins and vegetables second; garnishes last
4. **Use negative space deliberately** — do not fill the plate
5. **Step back** — evaluate from 60 cm away, which is roughly the diner's perspective

Review the plating composition section in [[culinary-patisserie/modules/09_desserts-and-plating]]
and the CHEATSHEET for the rule of thirds.
</details>

## Evaluation Criteria

There is no single correct tasting menu. Evaluate your own work against these criteria:

| Criterion | Excellent | Satisfactory | Needs Work |
|-----------|-----------|-------------|------------|
| **Flavour progression** | Light → rich → sweet arc is clear; each course invites the next | Some coherence; not all courses connect | Courses feel unrelated |
| **Technical execution** | Techniques from ≥3 modules applied correctly | Techniques attempted; minor errors | Major technique failures |
| **Sauce quality** | Correctly made, seasoned, and applied to at least 3 courses | Present in 2 courses | Absent or poorly executed |
| **Plating** | Deliberate composition; negative space; texture contrast | Presentable but ad hoc | Unmindful |
| **Documentation** | Complete recipe cards, cost calculations, photographs | Most documentation present | Minimal documentation |
| **Reflection** | Honest, specific, technically literate analysis of what worked and what to change | Present | Absent or vague |
