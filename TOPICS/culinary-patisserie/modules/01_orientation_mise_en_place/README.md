## Overview

This module develops practical patisserie judgment through clear vocabulary, repeatable workflow, and disciplined observation. The emphasis is on understanding why a technique works instead of memorizing isolated recipes.

You should finish with notes you can actually use in the kitchen: what you made, how you made it, what changed, what succeeded, and what you would test next.

## Prerequisites

- Previous module in this topic, unless this is Module 01.
- Basic arithmetic with grams, percentages, time, and temperature.
- Access to a clean kitchen workspace and common measuring tools.

## Objectives

By the end of this module, you will be able to:

- Explain the module vocabulary in practical kitchen language.
- Follow a simple professional workflow safely and repeatably.
- Record observations that support later troubleshooting.
- Connect technique choices to flavor, texture, and structure.

## Theory

### What This Module Covers

This module introduces how patisserie works, kitchen safety, tools, sanitation, workflow, and the discipline of preparation. Patisserie became a distinct professional craft because pastry work rewards precision: small differences in weight, temperature, mixing, resting, and timing can produce large differences in texture and structure. The purpose of this module is to build reliable habits before complexity increases.

### Core Mental Model

Think like a pastry cook by separating formula, method, environment, and evaluation. The formula tells you what goes in; the method tells you how energy and time are applied; the environment explains why today's dough or cream may behave differently; evaluation tells you what to adjust next time.

```yaml
production_note:
  formula: "ingredient weights and percentages"
  method: "mixing, resting, baking, cooling, finishing"
  environment: "room temperature, ingredient temperature, humidity"
  evaluation: "appearance, aroma, texture, flavor, structure"
```

### Practical Example

A simple production log prevents guessing. Even when making a basic component, write down enough information to repeat or improve it.

```text
Component: vanilla pastry cream
Milk: 500 g
Sugar: 100 g
Egg yolks: 90 g
Cornstarch: 40 g
Butter: 30 g
Method: cook to thick bubbling texture, whisk constantly, chill flat
Observation: smooth, slightly firm after 4 hours
Next test: reduce starch by 5 g for softer tart filling
```

### Why Precision Matters

Precision does not remove creativity; it protects it. When your base method is repeatable, you can adjust flavor, texture, format, and presentation intentionally. Without records, every success is hard to repeat and every failure is hard to diagnose.


## Key Concepts

- **Mise en place** — Preparing tools, ingredients, workspace, and timing before production begins.
- **Formula** — A weight-based description of a preparation that can be scaled and compared.
- **Method** — The ordered process that turns ingredients into a controlled result.
- **Sensory evaluation** — Structured judgment of aroma, flavor, texture, appearance, and finish.
- **Quality control** — The habit of defining success criteria before service or presentation.

## Examples

### Example: Component Planning Card

**Scenario:** You need to prepare a pastry component and leave enough information for a future repeat.

```yaml
component_card:
  name: "lemon curd"
  target_texture: "sliceable but creamy"
  critical_control: "gentle heat and constant stirring"
  cooling_method: "shallow pan, covered surface, refrigerator"
  quality_check: "glossy, no scrambled egg, bright acidity"
```

This card separates intention from execution. It tells you what success should look like and which step is most likely to cause failure.

## Common Pitfalls

### 1. Working before reading

**Mistake:** Starting a recipe before checking timing, tools, or cooling steps.

```text
Wrong approach: Starting a recipe before checking timing, tools, or cooling steps.
```

```text
Correct approach: Read the whole method, gather tools, and stage ingredients first.
```

This mistake usually happens when speed replaces method. Slow down, document the process, and connect the result to a specific cause.

### 2. Using vague quantities

**Mistake:** Measuring flour by a packed cup or a casual scoop.

```text
Wrong approach: Measuring flour by a packed cup or a casual scoop.
```

```text
Correct approach: Weigh ingredients in grams whenever possible.
```

This mistake usually happens when speed replaces method. Slow down, document the process, and connect the result to a specific cause.

### 3. Judging only flavor

**Mistake:** Calling a product successful because it tastes good once.

```text
Wrong approach: Calling a product successful because it tastes good once.
```

```text
Correct approach: Evaluate flavor, texture, structure, consistency, and repeatability.
```

This mistake usually happens when speed replaces method. Slow down, document the process, and connect the result to a specific cause.

## Summary

- Patisserie is a precision craft, not just a collection of recipes.
- Weight, temperature, timing, and method determine repeatability.
- Good notes make troubleshooting possible.
- Sensory evaluation should include texture and structure, not only flavor.
- Professional work balances quality, timing, cost, and service constraints.
