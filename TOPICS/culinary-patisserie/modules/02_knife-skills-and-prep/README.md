## Overview

Knife skill is the physical foundation of professional cooking. Before you can build a mirepoix,
prepare aromatics, or execute any technique that requires uniform cuts, you must be able to
handle a knife safely, efficiently, and precisely. This module covers the seven standard knife
cuts used in French classical and modern professional cooking — from the rough chop through the
delicate brunoise — and connects each cut to its practical culinary application.

Knife skill is not simply about speed; it is about uniformity. Uniformly cut vegetables cook at
the same rate, resulting in consistent texture. A 3 mm brunoise on a garnish speaks of training
and care; irregular lumpy "dices" speak of carelessness. The goal of knife practice is not to
cut as quickly as possible but to develop muscle memory that allows you to cut precisely without
conscious effort.

This module also covers the full mise en place workflow for prep work, aromatics preparation
(mirepoix, bouquet garni), and basic produce handling — the foundational prep work that underlies
every recipe in this topic.

## Objectives

By the end of this module, you will be able to:

1. Execute all seven standard knife cuts (chiffonade, mince, brunoise, small/medium/large dice, julienne, batonnet, tourné) with consistent dimensions
2. Demonstrate the correct pinch grip and claw grip for safe, controlled knife work
3. Prepare a classic mirepoix (onion, carrot, celery in 1:1:1 ratio) by medium dice
4. Produce a fine brunoise from any root vegetable
5. Sharpen a chef's knife using a honing steel and identify when a knife requires professional sharpening
6. Set up a complete prep station (*mise en place*) for a multi-component recipe before cooking begins

## Key Concepts

- **Chiffonade** — roll and slice leafy greens into thin ribbons; see [[culinary-patisserie#glossary]]
- **Brunoise** — 1–2 mm fine dice; the standard for fine garnishes and consommé
- **Julienne** — 3 × 3 × 60 mm matchstick; foundation for brunoise
- **Tourné** — a seven-sided barrel cut; the most difficult classical cut
- **Mirepoix** — the aromatic base of French cooking: onion, carrot, celery (2:1:1 by weight)
- **Bouquet garni** — tied bundle of herbs (bay leaf, thyme, parsley stalks) for flavouring stocks and braises
