# Exercises: Module 01 — Introduction to the Kitchen

## Instructions

Complete each exercise in order. Exercises increase in difficulty.
For culinary modules, exercises involve hands-on kitchen work, written analysis, or both.
Photograph your results where indicated — this builds a portfolio of your progress.

---

## Easy Exercises (1–3)

### Exercise 1
**Difficulty:** Easy
**Objective:** Practise mise en place discipline before a simple preparation

**Task:** Choose any simple recipe from your normal repertoire (a fried egg, a bowl of pasta,
a cup of tea). Before you start, set up a complete mise en place:
1. Read the "recipe" fully (even if you know it by heart — this is the habit)
2. Gather every piece of equipment
3. Measure and arrange every ingredient
4. Only then begin

**Reflection:** Write 3–5 sentences:
- How long did the mise en place take compared to the actual cooking?
- What did you discover you were missing or had to improvise?
- How did having everything ready change your experience of cooking?

---

### Exercise 2
**Difficulty:** Easy
**Objective:** Identify heat-transfer mechanisms in everyday cooking scenarios

**Task:** For each scenario below, identify the primary heat-transfer mechanism
(conduction, convection, or radiation) and explain why you chose it.

```text
Scenario A: Boiling potatoes in a large pot of salted water
Scenario B: Toasting bread under a grill/broiler
Scenario C: Pressing a panini in a contact sandwich press
Scenario D: Steaming broccoli in a basket above simmering water
Scenario E: Melting chocolate in a bowl set over a pot of barely simmering water (bain-marie)
```

Write one or two sentences per scenario explaining your reasoning.

### Exercise 3
**Difficulty:** Easy
**Objective:** Memorise the five basic tastes and apply them to real food

**Task:** Eat one piece of food that clearly represents each of the five basic tastes and
write a one-sentence description of the taste experience.

Examples (choose your own):
- Sweet: a ripe strawberry or a teaspoon of honey
- Salty: a plain cracker or a small piece of parmesan rind
- Sour: a wedge of lemon or plain yoghurt
- Bitter: unsweetened dark chocolate (70%+ cacao) or strong black coffee
- Umami: a spoonful of miso paste or a piece of aged cheddar

**Then:** Take a simple broth (stock cube is fine) and taste it. Add a few drops of lemon
juice and taste again. Write what changed. Then add a small pinch of salt and taste again.
Write what changed. This is acid and salt as balancing tools in real time.

## Medium Exercises (4–6)

### Exercise 4
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Objective:** Execute mise en place for a multi-component recipe

**Task:** Make a classic French vinaigrette using the following specification, with full
mise en place before any mixing begins.

**Classic Vinaigrette:**
- 45 ml extra-virgin olive oil
- 15 ml red wine vinegar
- 1 tsp Dijon mustard
- 1 small shallot (about 20 g), very finely minced
- Salt and freshly ground black pepper

**Steps:**
1. Set up mise en place: measure all liquids into separate small containers; mince
   shallot; lay out mustard, whisk, and medium bowl before starting
2. In the bowl, whisk mustard, vinegar, and salt
3. Add oil in a slow stream while whisking continuously to form an emulsion
4. Add shallot; taste and adjust seasoning
5. Dress a handful of mixed leaves; taste

**Written reflection:**
- Is the emulsion stable (does it hold, or does it separate immediately)?
- What happens if you add all the oil at once instead of gradually? Try it with a small
  amount and describe the difference.
- What would you change to make the vinaigrette more acidic? More savoury?

### Exercise 5
**Difficulty:** Medium
**Objective:** Practise kitchen safety — food temperature awareness

**Task:** Using a thermometer, measure and record the temperature of:
1. Your refrigerator (aim: ≤ 4 °C)
2. A cup of freshly boiled water after 1 minute, 5 minutes, and 10 minutes of cooling
3. A cup of water at "room temperature" (this is your baseline for the danger zone exercise)
4. A cooked piece of chicken (any method) at the thickest point before and after resting

**Record your readings in a table:**

| Item | Measured Temperature | Expected/Safe Range | Pass/Fail |
|------|---------------------|--------------------|-----------| 
| Refrigerator | | ≤ 4 °C | |
| Boiled water (1 min) | | ~90–99 °C | |
| Boiled water (5 min) | | ~70–85 °C | |
| Boiled water (10 min) | | ~55–70 °C | |
| Chicken at thickest point | | ≥ 74 °C | |

**Reflection:** At what point during cooling does the water enter the danger zone (below 60 °C)?
How quickly could a pot of hot stock enter the danger zone if left at room temperature?

## Hard Exercises (7–8)

### Exercise 7
**Difficulty:** Hard
**Objective:** Demonstrate the Maillard reaction and understand its requirements

**Task:** Cook the same ingredient (chicken breast or mushroom) four ways to observe the
effect of different conditions on browning:

```text
Trial 1 — Correct conditions:
  — Pat dry completely; preheat pan until very hot; cook undisturbed 3 min

Trial 2 — Wet surface:
  — Do NOT pat dry; place in the same hot pan; cook 3 min

Trial 3 — Pan not hot enough:
  — Pat dry; place in cold or barely warm pan; cook 3 min

Trial 4 — Overcrowded pan:
  — Pat dry; add TWO pieces to a pan designed for one; cook 3 min
```

**Photograph all four results side by side.**

**Written analysis (minimum 150 words):**
- Describe the colour, texture, and aroma of each result
- Explain the food science behind the different outcomes using the concepts from this module
- Which variable made the biggest difference?

### Exercise 8
**Difficulty:** Hard
**Objective:** Apply the brigade system as an organisational framework

**Task:** Plan a simple three-component meal: a protein, a vegetable side, and a sauce.
Before cooking:
1. Write a "brigade plan" — assign each component to a "station" and estimate its timing
2. Work backwards from your desired serving time to create a cooking timeline
3. Write your complete mise en place checklist
4. Execute the meal and note where the timing plan succeeded or failed

**Example template:**

```text
Meal: Roast chicken breast / Sautéed courgette / Pan jus

Station assignment:
  — Rôtisseur: Chicken breast (25 min oven + 5 min rest)
  — Entremetier: Courgette (10 min total, start 15 min after chicken enters oven)
  — Saucier: Pan jus from chicken pan (5 min, start while chicken rests)

Timeline (working backwards from 19:00 service):
  18:35 — Mise en place complete; oven preheated to 200 °C
  18:40 — Chicken seared and transferred to oven
  18:55 — Courgette started on hob
  19:00 — Chicken removed to rest; pan deglazed for jus
  19:05 — All components ready simultaneously; plate and serve
```

**Reflection:** Did everything come together at the same time? What would you change next time?

## Expert Exercise (9)

### Exercise 9
**Difficulty:** Expert
**Objective:** Synthesise heat science, flavour balance, and mise en place in a designed dish

**Task:** Design and cook an original one-plate dish that:
1. Deliberately uses two different heat-transfer mechanisms for different components
2. Contains at least four of the five basic tastes
3. Is executed with a complete written mise en place plan prepared in advance
4. Is plated with deliberate attention to visual composition (see Module 09 for plating theory)

**Document the dish with:**
- A written recipe with ingredients and procedure
- An annotated explanation of the heat science (which mechanism, why chosen, what result)
- A flavour map (list each component and which taste it contributes)
- One photograph of the finished plate

There is no single correct answer here. The dish can be simple or complex. The quality of the
reasoning matters more than the sophistication of the recipe.
