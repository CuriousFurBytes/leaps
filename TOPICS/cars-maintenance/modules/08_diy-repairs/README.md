## Theory

### Brake Pad Replacement — Full Procedure

Tools: Floor jack, jack stands, lug wrench, socket set (typically 10mm, 12mm, 13mm, 14mm), C-clamp or caliper piston tool, wire brush, brake cleaner, brake caliper lubricant (NOT grease on the pad friction surface).

Key concepts before starting: Which way does the caliper piston compress? (Most front calipers push straight in; some rear calipers must be wound in clockwise). Why must pads be bedded in? (New pads have a hard resin surface that must be heat-cycled off to expose the full friction compound).

Procedure: Remove wheel → inspect rotor → unclip pad wear indicator → remove caliper bolts → hang caliper (do not let it hang by the brake hose) → compress piston → clean caliper bracket → install new pads with correct hardware → apply lubricant to caliper sliding pins only → reinstall caliper → pump pedal before driving → bed-in procedure.

### Spark Plug Replacement

Gap specification: use feeler gauge; iridium and platinum plugs should not have gap adjusted. Anti-seize compound: recommended on aluminum heads. Torque: typically 12–18 lb-ft for most 14mm plugs. Firing order: matters for marking coil wires on older vehicles (direct-injection coil-on-plug ignition means no wires to confuse). Inspect old plugs for diagnostic information.

### Air Filter Replacement

Engine air filter location: typically a black plastic box connected to the intake tube. Most require one to four clips or screws — no tools on many vehicles. Inspect for rodent nests (especially in older or stored vehicles). Replace if visibly dirty or per interval.

Cabin air filter location: under glove box, behind glove box, or under dashboard depending on vehicle. Most replacement takes under 5 minutes. Heavy odors may require activated carbon filter upgrade.

### Windshield Wiper Replacement

Hook-type (most common): Press the release tab, rotate the blade, and slide it off. Verify replacement blade hook size. Pinch-tab and side-pin variants require slightly different technique — usually covered by a card in the replacement package.

### Exterior Bulb Replacement

Halogen headlights: touch glass with bare hands introduces oils that cause hot spots and premature failure — always use gloves or a cloth. Some vehicles require bumper removal for headlight access — research before attempting. LED and HID headlights: do not DIY on HID without training (high voltage capacitors can discharge lethally even unplugged). Turn signals and brake lights: typically accessible from the trunk (rear) or through the wheel well (front corner) by removing a small panel.

## Key Concepts

- **Bedding-in (brake break-in)**: Procedure of graduated heat cycles to transfer even layer of pad friction material onto rotor surface; prevents vibration and improves performance
- **Anti-seize compound**: Thread lubricant applied to spark plug threads in aluminum heads; prevents seizure and thread damage during future removal
- **Caliper piston wind-in**: Some rear brake calipers have integrated parking brake mechanisms requiring the piston to be wound clockwise (not just compressed) — doing it wrong damages the caliper
- **Double-gasketing**: Forgetting to remove old filter gasket before installing new one; causes leak
- **Brake cleaning**: Always clean rotors with brake cleaner spray before installing new pads — oil or grease on rotor surface causes immediate brake fade and squeal

## Common Pitfalls

- Not compressing the brake caliper piston before installing pads — pads won't fit and caliper won't close over the new (thicker) pads
- Not pumping the brake pedal before attempting to drive — first pump after reassembly moves pads back to rotor contact; driving without this has no brakes for the first stop
- Using grease instead of brake caliper lubricant on sliding pins — grease softens and runs onto friction surfaces; causes sticking calipers and squealing
- Touching halogen bulbs with bare hands — contamination causes localized overheating and early bulb failure
- Installing spark plugs without confirming gap — even "pre-gapped" plugs can be bumped during shipping

## Summary

- Brake pad replacement: compress piston, clean bracket, lubricate pins (not friction surfaces), torque caliper bolts, pump pedal, bed in with graduated stops
- Spark plug replacement: confirm gap, use anti-seize on aluminum heads, torque to spec, inspect old plugs for diagnostic clues
- Air and cabin filter: easy, no-tools jobs with significant impact on performance and air quality
- Wiper and bulb replacement: critical for visibility; handle halogens with gloves; some headlights require access work
- Safety first on every repair: lift safely, brake pedal pump before driving, verify no leaks before moving vehicle
