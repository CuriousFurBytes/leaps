## Overview

> [!NOTE]
> **This module is a stub.** The full theory, examples, exercises, test, and answer key are planned for a future expansion. The outline below defines what this module will cover when fully written.

Engine oil is the single most important fluid in your car. Understanding what oil does, how it degrades, how to select the right specification, and how to change it yourself will save you money and prevent the most common causes of premature engine wear. This module goes beyond the oil-check overview from Module 01 into full procedure detail.

Beyond oil, this module covers every other major fluid: coolant flushing, transmission fluid, brake fluid replacement, and filter maintenance (engine air filter, cabin air filter, and fuel filter).

**Difficulty:** Beginner &nbsp;|&nbsp; **Estimated time:** 4 hours

## Objectives

By the end of this module, you will be able to:

1. Explain the difference between conventional, synthetic blend, and full synthetic engine oil — and select the correct specification for your vehicle
2. Perform a complete oil and filter change safely, including proper torque on the drain plug and filter
3. Explain the degradation mechanisms of engine oil and why oil change intervals matter
4. Perform a coolant flush and refill with the correct coolant type and mixture ratio
5. Identify when transmission fluid, brake fluid, and power steering fluid need replacement
6. Replace engine and cabin air filters without tools
7. Interpret oil analysis results to assess engine health

## Theory

### Engine Oil: Types and Selection

Oil viscosity (e.g., 5W-30), SAE grades, the multi-grade system, and why synthetic oil lasts longer than conventional. How to read the API donut and ILSAC starburst symbols on oil containers. The difference between oil types and why using the wrong one matters.

### How Engine Oil Degrades

What actually happens to oil over time and heat: oxidation, nitration, soot buildup, additive depletion, and how TBN (Total Base Number) decreases. Why oil turns black and when black oil is vs. isn't a problem.

### Step-by-Step Oil Change Procedure

Complete procedure from warming the engine → lifting safely → draining → filter replacement → refilling → checking → cleanup. Specific torque values, filter orientation, gasket procedure, oil capacity by engine size.

### Coolant System Service

Coolant types (IAT green, OAT orange/pink, HOAT), mixing rules (never mix types), flush procedure, air bleeding, and why coolant intervals matter.

### Transmission, Brake, and Power Steering Fluid

When each needs service, how to check condition, service procedures and warnings for each.

### Air and Cabin Filters

How each type of filter affects engine performance and HVAC quality. Simple replacement procedures — no tools required for most vehicles.

## Key Concepts

- **Viscosity grade** (e.g., 5W-30): cold flow (5W) and hot flow (30) specifications
- **Synthetic vs. conventional oil**: base oil differences, additive packages, and interval implications
- **TBN (Total Base Number)**: the alkaline reserve that neutralizes combustion acids
- **OAT/HOAT coolant types**: organic acid technology vs. hybrid formulations — mixing compatibility
- **Filter bypass valve**: the safety mechanism that allows oil flow when a clogged filter would restrict it

## Common Pitfalls

- Using the wrong oil specification (e.g., 10W-40 where 0W-20 is specified) — consequences for VVT systems and fuel economy
- Over-tightening the drain plug or filter — stripped threads in aluminum pans
- Not lubricating the new filter's rubber gasket — can cause double-gasket sealing failure
- Mixing incompatible coolant types — gel formation that clogs the cooling system
- Forgetting to reinstall the drain plug before adding new oil (it happens to everyone once)
