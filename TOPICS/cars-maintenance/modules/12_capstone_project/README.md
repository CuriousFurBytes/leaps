## Overview

This capstone is build-oriented. You will not receive a copy-paste solution; instead, you will create a realistic maintenance program that a careful owner, family, or small fleet could actually use. The artifact may be a Markdown binder, printed checklist packet, spreadsheet, or mixed document set, but it must be understandable without special software.

The project synthesizes the full topic: inspection, fluids, tires, brakes, battery, diagnostics, records, cost planning, shop communication, modern vehicle constraints, and risk-ranked decision-making. The finished program should help someone maintain one specific vehicle safely over the next year.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> The capstone does not authorize high-risk repairs. It asks you to plan, inspect, document, and decide. Any task involving airbags, high voltage, fuel leaks, structural damage, brake hydraulics, steering, suspension, or unclear lift/support procedures belongs in the professional-help category unless you are properly trained.

## Prerequisites

- Modules 01 through 11 of this topic.
- Access to an owner manual or reliable sample vehicle information.
- Ability to distinguish owner checks from safety-critical repair procedures.

## Objectives

By the end of this module, you will be able to:

- Design a complete maintenance program for one vehicle.
- Rank maintenance tasks by safety risk, urgency, cost of delay, and owner suitability.
- Produce checklists that are concise enough to use in real life.
- Communicate findings and requests clearly to a repair shop.
- Defend your maintenance choices using evidence and vehicle-specific information.

## Project Brief

Choose one real or realistic vehicle. Build a maintenance program that covers the next 12 months or 12,000 miles, whichever comes first.

Your final artifact must include:

```text
vehicle-profile.md
safety-rules.md
monthly-inspection-checklist.md
service-schedule.md
maintenance-log.md
shop-communication-template.md
risk-register.md
budget-and-priorities.md
```

You may use different filenames, but the same information must be present.

## Milestones

### Milestone 1: Vehicle Profile

Identify the vehicle, information sources, operating conditions, tire specification, fluid references, known concerns, and assumptions.

### Milestone 2: Safety Rules

Write a one-page safety policy for the vehicle. Include lifting rules, PPE, stop-work triggers, high-risk systems, and emergency conditions.

### Milestone 3: Inspection and Service Plan

Create monthly, seasonal, pre-trip, and mileage-based checklists. Separate inspect, maintain, diagnose, and professional-service tasks.

### Milestone 4: Risk Register

List at least 15 possible issues. Rank each by safety risk, likelihood, cost of delay, owner suitability, and recommended next action.

### Milestone 5: Shop Communication Packet

Write a template for describing symptoms, recent work, test conditions, warning lights, and requested diagnostic boundaries.

### Milestone 6: Final Review

Audit your program for unsafe assumptions, missing specifications, vague tasks, and undocumented sources.

## Help and Getting Unstuck

### Hint 1: Start With What Can Hurt Someone

If you feel overwhelmed, first list every condition that means stop driving: brake warning light, oil pressure warning, overheating, fuel smell, severe tire damage, steering loss, grinding brakes, or unstable handling.

### Hint 2: Use a Simple Risk Matrix

```text
Risk score = safety severity + likelihood + cost of delay - owner suitability
High score: prioritize or refer to a professional.
Low score: monitor or schedule routine service.
```

The formula is only a thinking aid. Explain your judgment in words.

### Hint 3: Do Not Write Repair Instructions for Dangerous Work

For high-risk items, your program should say what to observe, what to record, when to stop driving, and what to ask a professional to inspect. It should not provide a complete procedure for brake hydraulic repair, airbag work, high-voltage isolation, fuel leak repair, or structural repair.

### Hint 4: Keep Checklists Short Enough to Use

A checklist nobody uses is not a maintenance program. Put detailed explanations in reference pages, and keep recurring inspection pages concise.

## Acceptance Criteria

- The program is vehicle-specific or clearly labels assumptions.
- Safety warnings are visible and practical.
- The maintenance schedule covers time, mileage, season, and condition-based checks.
- The risk register includes at least 15 issues with justified rankings.
- The shop communication template helps avoid vague complaints and parts-cannon diagnosis.
- The final artifact does not encourage unsafe work beyond the learner's training.

## Summary

- The capstone turns isolated maintenance skills into a real program.
- The deliverable emphasizes safety, evidence, records, prioritization, and communication.
- The learner drives the build; hints guide the work without replacing it.
- A good program helps decide what to do, what to monitor, and when to seek professional help.
- Professional judgment includes refusing unsafe shortcuts.
