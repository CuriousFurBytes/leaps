## Overview

This module frames home renovation as controlled change in a real building, not as decoration alone. You will practice turning observations into decisions while respecting safety, Brazilian climate, materials, and verification duties.

The focus is deliberately conservative: when the consequence of being wrong is injury, structural damage, fire, flooding, legal exposure, or expensive rework, the correct beginner move is to pause and verify with current sources and qualified professionals.

## Prerequisites

- Modules 01 through 13 of this topic.
- Basic measurement, photography, note-taking, and spreadsheet skills.
- A commitment to verify Brazilian standards, municipal rules, and professional sign-off requirements before field work.

## Objectives

By the end of this module, you will be able to:

- Explain the main risks and decision points for this stage of renovation.
- Create a written checklist that separates facts, assumptions, and verification tasks.
- Identify when Brazilian climate, materials, or standards may change the correct choice.
- Communicate clearly with homeowners, contractors, condominium managers, and licensed professionals.
- Produce a small portfolio artifact suitable for review.

## Theory

### Project Brief

Design a complete renovation plan for a real or realistic Brazilian residence. The artifact is not a copy-paste construction manual; it is a decision package that a homeowner, architect, engineer, contractor, or condominium board could review. It must synthesize safety, diagnosis, climate, materials, systems, contracts, maintenance, and verification checkpoints from the whole topic.

### Required Deliverables

Your capstone must include an existing-condition dossier, scope narrative, risk register, rough budget, schedule, trade coordination plan, standards verification log, maintenance calendar, and handover checklist. Use placeholders when the exact Brazilian standard, municipal rule, or professional sign-off requirement has not been verified.

```yaml
project:
  city_state: "[fill in]"
  building_type: "apartment | house | mixed"
  climate_exposures:
    - "humidity"
    - "intense rain"
  professional_signoffs:
    - "[Verify ART/RRT need with licensed professional]"
  non_negotiables:
    - "do not alter structure without qualified design"
    - "do not energize unfinished electrical circuits"
```

### Help and Getting Unstuck

Use staged hints before asking for a full review. The purpose is to keep you driving the project while giving enough structure to continue safely.

#### Checkpoint Hints

1. If scope feels vague, separate must-fix defects from desired upgrades.
2. If budget feels impossible, price demolition, waterproofing, electrical, plumbing, finishes, contingency, and professional fees separately.
3. If standards feel uncertain, write a verification placeholder instead of guessing.

```text
Verification log example:
- Item: Bathroom waterproofing
- Assumption: Full wet-area membrane system required before tile
- Verify with: Current ABNT standard, manufacturer manual, qualified professional
- Decision blocked until: Product system and detail are selected
```

## Key Concepts

- **Scope control** — The boundary between included and excluded work. A clear scope prevents hidden assumptions and makes contractor bids comparable.
- **Verification placeholder** — A written marker for requirements that must be checked before action. It prevents hallucinated standards and keeps legal or technical uncertainty visible.
- **Stop-work trigger** — A condition that makes continuing unsafe until a qualified person reviews the situation. Examples include gas odor, electric shock, active leaks near wiring, and structural movement.
- **Climate exposure** — Local stress such as coastal salt air, humidity, intense rain, heat, termites, or flooding. Good material choices depend on these exposures.
- **Lifecycle maintenance** — The planned inspections and small repairs that protect the renovation after handover. Maintenance is part of the design, not an afterthought.

## Examples

### Example 1: Risk Register Entry

**Scenario:** A bathroom renovation reveals damp masonry behind tile.

```yaml
risk:
  description: "Hidden moisture behind bathroom tile"
  likely_causes:
    - "failed waterproofing"
    - "pipe leak"
    - "condensation or exterior infiltration"
  action: "pause finishes until source is diagnosed"
  verify:
    - "current waterproofing standard and product manual"
    - "licensed professional review if structural or recurring"
```

This entry avoids guessing. It records uncertainty and blocks finish work until the source is known.

### Example 2: Material Selection Note

**Scenario:** Selecting fasteners for a coastal balcony repair.

```text
Use corrosion-resistant fasteners appropriate for coastal exposure.
Do not substitute indoor screws because they are cheaper.
[Verify exposure class, structural role, and manufacturer recommendation before purchase.]
```

The tradeoff is cost versus durability and safety. Coastal corrosion can turn a cheap shortcut into failure.

## Common Pitfalls

### Pitfall 1: Starting demolition before documentation

Wrong approach:

```text
Remove tiles first and decide later where the pipes are.
```

Correct approach:

```text
Photograph, scan where possible, shut off utilities, mark assumptions, and open small inspection areas before broad demolition.
```

This mistake happens because demolition feels productive. Documentation is slower at first but prevents broken pipes, electrical hazards, and disputes.

### Pitfall 2: Treating standards as memory

Wrong approach:

```text
I think the standard says this dimension is acceptable, so specify it.
```

Correct approach:

```text
[Verify current ABNT NBR, municipal code, condominium rule, and professional sign-off requirement before specifying.]
```

Standards and local rules can change. A placeholder is safer than a confident but unverified number.

### Pitfall 3: Buying finish materials before solving moisture

Wrong approach:

```text
Order premium flooring while the wall still has active damp stains.
```

Correct approach:

```text
Diagnose moisture source, repair root cause, allow drying, verify substrate condition, then purchase compatible finishes.
```

The visible finish is not the system. Moisture failures usually punish rushed sequencing.

## Summary

- Renovation decisions must separate facts, assumptions, and verification tasks.
- Brazil-specific work requires attention to humidity, rain, coastal corrosion, termites, local rules, and professional responsibility.
- Safety hazards outrank schedule and aesthetics.
- Standards-as-verification placeholders prevent false precision.
- Good documentation protects people, budget, quality, and future maintenance.
