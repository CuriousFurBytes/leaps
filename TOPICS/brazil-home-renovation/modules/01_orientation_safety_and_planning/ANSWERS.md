# Answers: Module 01 — Orientation, Safety, and Planning

## Answer Key

### Easy Questions
**Q1:** A written marker for a requirement that must be checked in current official or professional sources before action.
**Q2:** Examples include gas odor, electric shock, warm breaker, structural movement, active leak near wiring, fall risk, or unknown hazardous material.
**Q3:** Examples include humidity, intense rain, coastal salt corrosion, heat, termites, flooding, and mold risk.
**Q4:** Photos preserve evidence, support diagnosis, help bids, reduce disputes, and guide future maintenance.
**Q5:** A risk register records hazards, consequences, mitigations, owners, and verification tasks.

### Medium Questions
**Q6:** Standards and laws change, local rules vary, and exact clauses matter; verification prevents unsafe or legally wrong assumptions.
**Q7:** Cosmetic work changes appearance; structural, electrical, gas, waterproofing, facade, or layout work may affect safety or legal compliance and can require qualified review.
**Q8:** Humidity affects drying, adhesion, mold risk, corrosion, and finish durability, so wet causes must be solved before covering substrates.
**Q9:** It can limit work hours, noise, elevator use, waste routes, facade changes, wet-area changes, and approval steps.
**Q10:** Maintenance protects systems after handover by scheduling inspections, cleaning, resealing, leak checks, and documentation updates.

### Hard Questions
**Q11:** Strong answers include documentation, utility shutoff, hazard scan, moisture diagnosis, verification placeholders, professional review triggers, and sequencing.
**Q12:** The plan sequences demolition and purchasing before diagnosis. Fix by documenting, isolating utilities, investigating moisture and systems, verifying requirements, then buying compatible finishes.
**Q13:** Strong entries identify shock/fire risk, de-energizing, electrician review, load/circuit verification, and no continued use until resolved.
**Q14:** Strong answers include corrosion-resistant fasteners, waterproofing/drainage, facade/condominium rules, fall hazards, concrete cover/rebar corrosion, and wind-driven rain.

### Expert Questions
**Q15:** Strong workflows triage hazards, gather documents, require professional sign-off for structure/electrical/waterproofing, create scope, verify standards, schedule trades, inspect hold points, and document handover.
**Q16:** Strong processes integrate risk ranking, climate exposure, budget contingency, verification log, authority checks, approval gates, stop-work triggers, and maintenance planning.

### Bonus Questions
**Bonus 1:** Accept official or professional sources such as ABNT catalogs, CREA/CAU, municipal building department, Corpo de Bombeiros where applicable, concessionária, condominium administration, or qualified licensed professionals.
