# Home Renovation Brazil — Projects

> Project-based learning cements knowledge in a way that reading and exercises alone cannot.
> Building something real forces you to confront gaps in your understanding and make decisions
> that tutorials never put in front of you.

---

## How to Use This File

1. Choose a project at or slightly above your current level.
2. **Don't use tutorials for your chosen project.** Tutorials are for learning; projects are for applying.
3. Document your attempt using the [Project Attempt Template](#project-attempt-template) at the bottom.
4. When you finish, link the result (photos, document, plan) in the table in the topic README.

## Beginner Projects

_Focus: get comfortable with the core vocabulary, tools, and planning process._
_Complete at least one before advancing past Module 03._

### B1: Room Renovation Plan

**Description:** Choose a real room in your home (or a hypothetical room with specific dimensions)
and produce a complete renovation plan: a basic *croqui* (sketch floor plan with dimensions),
list of all materials needed with quantities calculated, and a budget estimate using SINAPI
or local supplier prices.

**Concepts Reinforced:**
- Reading and producing a basic floor plan
- Material quantity calculation (floor area, wall area, tile quantities with waste factor)
- Budget estimation using SINAPI or supplier catalogs
- Identifying DIY vs. professional tasks

**Estimated Time:** 6–10 hours

**Starting Point:** Any room in your home, or use this hypothetical: a 3.0 m × 3.5 m bedroom
needing new floor tiles, painted walls, and a new electrical outlet.

**Success Criteria:**
- [ ] Floor plan sketch drawn to scale (or clearly dimensioned)
- [ ] Material list covers all major items (tiles, grout, adhesive, paint, primer)
- [ ] Quantities calculated correctly with 10% waste factor for tiles
- [ ] Budget estimate produced with price sources noted
- [ ] DIY vs. professional tasks clearly identified

---

### B2: Tools Inventory and Gap Analysis

**Description:** Create a complete inventory of the tools available to you for home renovation
work. For each module in this topic, identify which tools are required, which you already have,
and which you would need to buy or rent. Produce a prioritized shopping/rental list with costs.

**Concepts Reinforced:**
- Understanding what tools are needed for each type of work
- Buy vs. rent decision-making for infrequently used tools
- Understanding safety equipment requirements (PPE)

**Estimated Time:** 3–5 hours

**Starting Point:** Read the tool lists in Modules 01, 06, 07, and 08 before starting.

**Success Criteria:**
- [ ] Inventory covers all tools needed for at least 6 modules
- [ ] Buy vs. rent recommendation made for each tool costing over R$100
- [ ] PPE list is complete (safety glasses, gloves, dust mask, hearing protection)
- [ ] Total investment estimate produced

## Intermediate Projects

_Focus: combine multiple concepts, apply material calculation formulas in a real context._
_Complete at least one before advancing past Module 07._

### I1: Bathroom Renovation Specification

**Description:** Produce a complete technical specification for a bathroom renovation.
Choose a real or hypothetical bathroom (provide dimensions). The specification must cover:
waterproofing (*impermeabilização*) design, tile layout plan (drawn on graph paper or similar),
hydraulic points to be maintained or moved, and an itemized bill of materials.

**Concepts Reinforced:**
- Impermeabilização design and product selection
- Tile layout planning (accounting for cuts, centering, doorway alignment)
- Integration of hydraulic and electrical considerations
- Specification writing (product names, quantities, application instructions)

**Estimated Time:** 12–16 hours

**Starting Point:** Use a real or hypothetical bathroom of approximately 2.5 m × 2.0 m.
You will need to apply concepts from Modules 05, 07, 08, and 09.

**Success Criteria:**
- [ ] Waterproofing specification includes product, number of coats, and cure time between coats
- [ ] Tile layout drawn on grid, showing cut patterns and centering logic
- [ ] Bill of materials itemizes every product with quantity and unit price
- [ ] Specification identifies all tasks requiring licensed professionals (plumber, electrician)
- [ ] Document is clear enough that a contractor could price from it

---

### I2: Electrical Circuit Audit

**Description:** Map the electrical installation of an existing space (your home or a family
member's). Identify all circuits, their breaker ratings, the loads connected to each, and
whether the installation is compliant with NBR 5410. Produce a written audit report with
findings and recommendations.

**Concepts Reinforced:**
- Circuit identification and mapping
- Load calculation per circuit (total wattage vs. breaker capacity)
- NBR 5410 compliance checking (circuit separation, GFCI requirements for wet areas, grounding)
- Risk assessment and prioritization of recommended improvements

**Estimated Time:** 8–12 hours

**Starting Point:** You will need the concepts from Module 04. You will need a non-contact
voltage tester (*detector de tensão*) and a pen and paper. Do not open panels or touch wiring.

**Success Criteria:**
- [ ] All circuits identified and labeled (lighting, sockets, shower, AC, etc.)
- [ ] Load calculation completed for each circuit
- [ ] At least one compliance issue identified and explained
- [ ] Recommendations are prioritized by safety impact
- [ ] Report explicitly flags any issues requiring a licensed electrician

---

### I3: Material Specification for a Kitchen Renovation

**Description:** Produce a full material specification for a kitchen renovation including:
new floor and wall tiles (with layout plan), countertop material selection and justification,
cabinet design sketch, and a list of all plumbing work required. Research and compare three
competing product options for each major material category.

**Concepts Reinforced:**
- Material selection criteria (durability, maintenance, cost, aesthetics)
- Comparing products across quality tiers
- Integration of hydraulic planning with finish work
- Cost-benefit analysis of material choices

**Estimated Time:** 12–18 hours

**Starting Point:** Use a real or hypothetical kitchen of approximately 3.5 m × 4.0 m.

**Success Criteria:**
- [ ] Three product options compared for tiles, countertop, and cabinet finish
- [ ] Tile layout plan produced with quantities calculated
- [ ] All plumbing work required identified and costed
- [ ] Final specification clearly separates DIY vs. contracted work
- [ ] Total budget estimate produced

## Advanced Projects

_Focus: tackle realistic, open-ended renovation problems involving multiple systems._

### A1: Complete Renovation Plan for a Small Apartment

**Description:** Produce a complete renovation plan for a 45–60 m² apartment.
The plan must include: architectural floor plan before and after (even a hand-drawn *croqui* is
acceptable), complete electrical redesign with circuit layout, hydraulic system mapping,
material specifications for all finishes, itemized budget, and a phased execution timeline
showing what work must be done in what order and why.

**Why This Is Hard:**
Real renovation projects require systems to be coordinated — you cannot tile walls before
electrical and hydraulic rough-in is done. You must understand the dependencies between
trades and sequence the work correctly. Budget and timeline must be realistic, not wishful.

**Concepts Reinforced:**
- Systems integration (electrical, hydraulic, structural, finishes must be coordinated)
- Work sequencing and trade dependencies
- Realistic cost estimation
- Risk identification (what could go wrong? what contingencies are needed?)
- Contractor briefing and scope-of-work writing

**Estimated Time:** 20–30 hours

**Starting Point:** You will need concepts from all of Modules 01–09. Use a real apartment
or a hypothetical floor plan (one will be provided in Module 12).

**Success Criteria:**
- [ ] Before/after floor plan clearly shows scope of changes
- [ ] Electrical layout shows all circuits, outlet positions, and panel design
- [ ] Hydraulic layout shows all supply and drain lines
- [ ] Material specification covers every finish surface (floors, walls, ceilings)
- [ ] Budget includes materials, labor, professional fees, and a 15% contingency
- [ ] Execution timeline is phased with correct trade sequencing
- [ ] You can explain every design decision you made

---

### A2: Rooftop Laje Waterproofing Specification

**Description:** Design a complete waterproofing (*impermeabilização*) system for a flat
concrete roof (*laje de cobertura*). The specification must cover: substrate assessment and
preparation, waterproofing product selection (flexible membrane, rigid coating, or asphalt
sheet), application procedure, drainage design (slope requirements per ABNT), and a maintenance
schedule with inspection checklist.

**Why This Is Hard:**
Waterproofing failures are the most common and expensive construction pathology in Brazil.
A correct specification requires understanding substrate conditions, movement joints, thermal
cycling, chemical compatibility, and how to detail penetrations (pipes, drains, vents) — the
details where most failures occur.

**Concepts Reinforced:**
- Impermeabilização product selection and compatibility
- Drainage slope design (minimum 1% per ABNT)
- Detailing of penetrations, flashings, and movement joints
- Long-term maintenance planning

**Estimated Time:** 15–20 hours

**Success Criteria:**
- [ ] Substrate assessment protocol is written step by step
- [ ] Product selection is justified with specific product datasheets referenced
- [ ] All penetrations and terminations are detailed in the specification
- [ ] Drainage design confirms minimum slope is maintained
- [ ] Maintenance schedule includes inspection checklist at 6-month intervals

## Expert / Capstone Projects

_Focus: synthesize everything. Produce a professional-quality document or real-world deliverable._

### E1: Full Renovation Project Package for a Residential Property

**Description:** This is the capstone of the entire topic. Produce a complete, professional-quality
renovation project package for a real or hypothetical residential property. The package must be
detailed enough that a licensed engineer or architect could review it, a contractor could price it,
and a bank (e.g., Caixa CEF) could evaluate it for a *crédito para reforma* loan.

**Why This Is a Capstone:**
This project requires mastery of all major topic areas. You cannot complete it with gaps —
they will surface immediately. Expect to revisit earlier modules during the build. The quality
of real renovation planning is not measured by knowledge of individual skills but by how well
you integrate them into a coherent, executable plan.

**Core Requirements:**
- [ ] Complete *memorial descritivo* (project description document) covering all renovation scope
- [ ] Architectural before/after floor plans (to scale, dimensioned)
- [ ] Electrical installation design (circuit layout, load calculation, panel specification)
- [ ] Hydraulic installation design (supply and drainage layout)
- [ ] Complete itemized *orçamento* (budget) using SINAPI reference prices with +15% contingency
- [ ] Phased execution plan with trade sequencing and timeline
- [ ] Contractor briefing document (*caderno de encargos*) covering quality standards required
- [ ] Covers concepts from at least 8 different modules
- [ ] Document is readable by someone without construction expertise (clear writing, defined terms)

**Estimated Time:** 25–40 hours

**Extension Ideas (for more challenge):**
- Include a solar energy system design as per ANEEL micro-generation rules
- Add an accessibility (*acessibilidade*, NBR 9050) audit and upgrade plan
- Develop a sustainability rating using the AQUA-HQE or PROCEL Edifica framework

> [!NOTE]
> The final module of this topic (Module 12: Capstone Project) is a dedicated guided walkthrough
> of this project. It provides milestones, checkpoints, and a **Help / Getting Unstuck** section
> with staged hints — but it deliberately does **not** hand you a finished document. The help
> is there so you can get past a blocker and keep going on your own. Struggling through the
> decisions is the learning here.
